Volume I — Ten passages from sound to recognition

The strongest approach is to make this a graded Sanskrit apprenticeship disguised as a beautiful book of prayers and philosophical revelations.

You begin with a handful of sounds. By the end, you're reading an actual sentence of Abhinavagupta's Tantrasāra, understanding how its grammar produces its meaning.

I checked the Sanskrit of the Bhairavastava, Anuttarāṣṭikā, Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā, Bodhapañcadaśikā, Paramārthasāra and Tantrasāra. They provide enough material for a coherent first volume without introducing quotations from other authors.

# 5 Learning stages

The crucial pedagogical decision is discovery before explanation. The learner observes a Sanskrit form, predicts how it works, tests that prediction against other passages, and gradually internalises the pattern.

But the mathematical precision you want remains available in every lesson. No lengthy detached grammar lectures; instead, exact derivation proofs attached to the words being learned.

## Before lesson one: install the Sanskrit alphabet

I wouldn't require anyone to memorise Devanāgarī before starting. I'd introduce all the sounds immediately, then allow the script to become familiar through repeated reading and writing.

The first pages should contain:

- A permanent two-page IAST ↔ Devanāgarī reference sheet, organised by how sounds are produced in the mouth.
- The independent vowels and dependent vowel signs, especially the inherent a: क = ka, कि = ki, का = kā, क् = k.
- Aspirated/unaspirated and dental/retroflex pairs, including न (na) versus ण (ṇa).
- An introduction to the Mātṛkā alphabet and Abhinavagupta's phonemic cosmology, with that spiritual interpretation carefully distinguished from ordinary phonetics.

### Incorporate your existing body-placement app

Mātṛkā Nyāsa — phoneme and body practice

An optional companion to the first lessons, using the Sanskrit sounds, articulation and traditional body placements.

For example, begin with the distinction between अ (a) and आ (ā). Hear them, pronounce them, trace them, then encounter the same distinction in the first grammatical transformation.

The traditional nyāsa practice should remain optional, not a prerequisite for literacy. The book can link to the practice while keeping grammatical correctness independent of ritual visualisation.

### The script-transition strategy

Lessons 1–2 — IAST first: Devanāgarī beside it. Trace a few characters and reconstruct them with the reference sheet.

Lessons 3–4 — Equal prominence: read both scripts, then attempt to write the Sanskrit before looking at the Devanāgarī.

Lessons 5–7 — Devanāgarī first: try reading the script directly. Reveal IAST only to check your reading.

Lessons 8–10 — Independent reading: start with Devanāgarī, read aloud, decode the sentence, then verify against the full analysis.

IAST never becomes forbidden. You simply find yourself needing it less.

The reference sheet should be available on every page as a foldout or endpaper. For a digital version, tapping any character should reveal its sound, articulation and component strokes.

### Stage I — The alphabet begins making sense

Lessons 1–2 · First sounds, first sandhi, first sentences

| # | Authentic Sanskrit selection | New language skill |
| - | ---------------------------- | ------------------ |
| 1 | mamātmā — Bhairavastava 2 | a + ā → ā (Pāṇini 6.1.101 akaḥ savarṇe dīrghaḥ). Start from mama + ātmā. Inherent a, long ā, first vowel contraction |
| 2 | namaste viśvamūrtaye — Mahopadeśa 1 | namas + te, "to you", dative case, first simple compound; plus namaḥ → namo before n (from Mahopadeśa 2 namo namaḥ) |

Lesson 1 should be genuinely delightful. You discover that two words have apparently fused:

THE FIRST DERIVATION

मम — mama — my

+

आत्मा — ātmā — Self

# ममात्मा — mamātmā — "My Self"

That's a complete first lesson in sound, writing, meaning and Sanskrit word formation, with only one new sandhi rule.

The formal reference is Pāṇini 6.1.101, akaḥ savarṇe dīrghaḥ. We can show the actual derivation without requiring the beginner to memorise that technical formulation.

### Stage II — Sanskrit begins behaving like a language

Lessons 3–4 · Agreement, verbs, location

| # | Authentic Sanskrit selection | New language skill |
| - | ---------------------------- | ------------------ |
| 3 | hṛdi vande — Bhairavastava 1 | "I praise [Bhairava] in the heart." Locative case and first-person present verb |
| 4 | nṛtyati gāyati hṛṣyati gāḍham — Bhairavastava 9 | "Dances, sings, rejoices intensely." Three present-tense verbs exhibiting the same ending; discover -ti before the chart |

Lesson 4: ask the learner what nṛtyati — gāyati — hṛṣyati have in common before revealing the grammar and the subject saṃvid iyaṃ — "this consciousness" — from the next line. Philosophy: consciousness itself dancing, singing, rejoicing.

### Stage V — Read Abhinavagupta in the original

Lessons 9–10 · Complete verse and philosophical prose

| # | Full reading | Final challenge |
| -- | ------------ | --------------- |
| 9 | Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā 8 — the complete "Enough of difference-talk" verse | Recover all four quarters, explain tvadbhaktirasacarvaṇāt, recognise the infinitive vaktuṃ and the verb lajjate |
| 10 | Tantrasāra, Āhnika 1 — opening prose argument: iha jñānaṃ mokṣakāraṇaṃ bandhanimittasya ajñānasya virodhakatvāt | Genitive constructions, abstract nominal formations, ablative of cause, reading without metrical structure |

The learner identifies what causes liberation, what causes bondage, and how Sanskrit establishes that causal relationship (virodhakatvāt = ablative of cause).

That is a meaningful progression from mamātmā to philosophical prose.

## The learning experience inside each chapter

1. **Hear and enjoy** — original Sanskrit recited correctly; whole passage + natural translation; why someone would remember it.
2. **Discover the pattern** — elements side by side; what changed? Work it out before the answer.
3. **Prove the construction** — exact word boundaries, root/stem, suffix, case, sandhi, syntax. Technical grammar only after the pattern. Forward then reverse.
4. **Write and read** — Devanāgarī via reference sheet; compare; eventually read without IAST.
5. **Understand the Trika meaning** — reassemble literal meaning; philosophical explanation; distinguish Abhinavagupta's words from our interpretation. Entertaining slang readings only after the grammar.
6. **Memorise and transfer** — recite, write, then an unseen grammatical problem.

## Rule boxes (owner 2026-11-07)

Simple **rule boxes** for things to remember — mathematical, foundational (like a+a→ā). Do not overwhelm: **a handful of boxes in the whole volume**, introduced when the pattern first appears, then short reminders only.

## Grammar coverage audit (10 lessons)

| Grammatical system | Volume I target |
| ------------------ | --------------- |
| Phonology | vowels, length, aspiration, retroflexion, anusvāra, visarga |
| Script | akṣaras, vowel signs, conjuncts, virāma (reference sheet) |
| Sandhi | a+a→ā, visarga→r/n/m, yaṇ a+e→ai, namaḥ→namo |
| Nouns | case functions in practice (nom, acc, instr, dat, loc, abl, gen) |
| Number and gender | singular + **dual** (L7); m/f/n visible |
| Pronouns | aham, tvam, tad, idam forms in context |
| Verbs | present -ti, middle, imperatives, prohibition mā |
| Non-finite | participle, infinitive vaktuṃ, gerundive introduced lightly |
| Compounds | tatpuruṣa, karmadhāraya, bahuvrīhi samples |
| Syntax | agreement, nominal sentences, case roles |
| Metre | hear verse rhythm; no forced scansion of all |

Three encounters minimum per feature where feasible: introduce, contrast, independent test. Any newly constructed drill sentence is **labelled exercise**, never quoted as Abhinavagupta.

## Final examination

Unseen authentic passage (reserve Bodhapañcadaśikā 14). Identify words, agreement, sandhi, known case endings, provisional translation; answer key with exact derivations.

Assess five things: pronunciation; Devanāgarī from marked IAST; reverse sandhi + inflected forms; infer grammar before translation; distinguish literal Sanskrit from philosophical interpretation.

## Organising principle

Sound → Word → Rule → Sentence → Meaning → Recognition — then return to the same Sanskrit with greater understanding.

The book starts with the learner discovering what happens when mama and ātmā meet: mamātmā, "my Self". It ends with Abhinavagupta's discussion of how knowledge removes ignorance in the Tantrasāra.

Keep the lesson spine fixed; individual verse excerpts may be revised during textual review if another authentic Abhinavagupta passage teaches the same grammatical pattern more naturally. **The grammatical progression is the spine; the poetry is how we bring it to life.**
