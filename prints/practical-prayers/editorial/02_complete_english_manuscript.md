## How to read a prayer
The complete original verse is always the authority. The smaller phrase is a memory handle, not necessarily a historically transmitted mantra. Do not measure prayer by heat, visions, unusual energy or mood changes. Complete the action and return to your day.

## Anytime

### A bow in five syllables  (`om-namah-shivaya`)
**Source:** Pañcākṣara mantra.
**Recitation:** FULL.
**Pocket words:** *oṃ namaḥ śivāya* — candidate excerpt; source-check before printing.
**Sense:** Oṃ; homage to Śiva. The mantra addresses and honours Śiva; Trika invites us also to discover that the one invoking is not ultimately separate from the invoked.
**When:** At a threshold: standing up, leaving a room, or noticing that attention has become scattered.
**What to do:** Say the entire mantra once, at a normal pace. A slight bow of the head is enough; no counting is required.
**Devotional recognition:** Treat this as offering the next moment, not requesting a special sensation.
**Return:** Do the next ordinary thing carefully.
**Editorial check (do not print):** Do not break namaḥ into naḥ·maḥ; the five-syllable count is na–ma–śi–vā–ya, preceded here by oṃ.

### An invocation of Aghora  (`bah-gayatri`)
**Source:** Bahurūpa-Garbha Stotra, Gāyatrī.
**Recitation:** FULL / COLLATION HOLD.
**Pocket words:** *oṃ bahurūpāya vidmahe koṭarākṣāya dhīmahi tan no ’ghoraḥ pracodayāt* — candidate excerpt; source-check before printing.
**Sense:** The Gāyatrī asks that Aghora direct or inspire understanding. The epithet koṭarākṣa needs a secure gloss from the textual tradition.
**When:** When you deliberately want to begin a devotional recitation, not as an emergency shortcut.
**What to do:** Recite it calmly once, or three times if following the supplied ritual sequence. Do not require breath holds or body sensations.
**Devotional recognition:** The act is asking for guidance, not proving that a deity has intervened.
**Return:** Return to the task with a more careful intention.
**Editorial check (do not print):** CHECK source spelling bahurūpāya (long ū), and the meaning of koṭarākṣa; do not print the unsubstantiated “hundred-thousand-eyed” gloss.

**When:** At the end of a deliberate ritual or an evening recitation.

## Wake

### The world is the form of the Lord  (`mpa-1`)
**Source:** Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā 1.
**Recitation:** POCKET + FULL.
**Pocket words:** *namaste viśvamūrtaye* — candidate excerpt; source-check before printing.
**Sense:** Homage to the One whose form is the universe, yet who is beyond its manifest forms.
**When:** On first opening the eyes, before touching the phone.
**What to do:** Look once at whatever is actually there—ceiling, light, window. Recite the short invocation once, or the complete verse if learned.
**Devotional recognition:** The room need not become extraordinary; it is included in what is addressed.
**Return:** Get up and attend to the day.
**Editorial check (do not print):** The four-word pocket line is an excerpt from a longer verse; make that visible.

### This body is a shrine  (`deha-1`)
**Source:** Traditional deho devālayaḥ verse; attribution disputed.
**Recitation:** HALF-VERSE / ATTRIBUTION HOLD.
**Pocket words:** *deho devālayaḥ proktaḥ svātmā devaḥ sanātanaḥ* — candidate excerpt; source-check before printing.
**Sense:** The body is called the deity’s temple, and one’s Self the enduring deity. This is a traditional maxim; its attribution as verse 1 of Abhinavagupta’s Dehasthadevatācakra is not supported by the checked witness.
**When:** While still in bed or just sitting up.
**What to do:** Be aware of the whole body, without requiring it to feel calm or sacred. Say the half-verse slowly.
**Devotional recognition:** Respect the body as a place of worship, including when tired, ordinary or uncomfortable.
**Return:** Stand, wash, and care for it.
**Editorial check (do not print):** Keep as unattributed/traditional only if a publication-quality witness and rights are secured; otherwise replace with a genuinely sourced Dehastha verse.

### Waking is one of the Lord’s costumes  (`mpa-10`)
**Source:** Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā 10.
**Recitation:** POCKET + FULL.
**Pocket words:** *niṣkalo ’si ca tattvataḥ* — candidate excerpt; source-check before printing.
**Sense:** Waking, dreaming, and deep sleep are described as expressions of the Lord, who is nevertheless partless in reality.
**When:** At the transition out of sleep.
**What to do:** Name the state you have just left, then say the short clause “yet in truth you are without parts.”
**Devotional recognition:** Recognise a change of state without needing to locate an additional permanent mental object.
**Return:** Begin ordinary morning activity.
**Editorial check (do not print):** Do not teach the waking/dreaming metaphor as empirical proof of metaphysical identity.

### The Goddess makes the world appear  (`deha-6`)
**Source:** Dehasthadevatācakrastotra, printed as 6; witness verse 5.
**Recitation:** HALF-VERSE / RENUMBER.
**Pocket words:** *ānandabhairavīṃ tāṃ vimarśarūpām ahaṃ vande* — candidate excerpt; source-check before printing.
**Sense:** The hymn praises Ānandabhairavī, whose reflective awareness is linked with the world’s appearing, shining, and being relished.
**When:** At dawn or the first visible change in daylight.
**What to do:** Notice light arriving and recite the closing praise of the Goddess. No visualisation is necessary.
**Devotional recognition:** What appears and the knowing of it can be contemplated together.
**Return:** Open the curtains or leave the room.
**Editorial check (do not print):** Independent witness numbers this verse 5 rather than 6; use its reading udāya...līlāṃ, and collate the Sanskrit.

## Bath

### What bathing can the stainless need?  (`mpa-14`)
**Source:** Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā 14.
**Recitation:** POCKET + FULL.
**Pocket words:** *nirmalasya kutaḥ snānam* — candidate excerpt; source-check before printing.
**Sense:** The Lord is already stainless. Why would a bath purify the one who is beyond impurity?
**When:** While washing hands or showering.
**What to do:** Ask the rhetorical question once and wash normally.
**Devotional recognition:** The water cares for a body, while the verse challenges the idea that the absolute needs cleaning.
**Return:** Dry yourself and leave.
**Editorial check (do not print):** Keep ritual paradox separate from ordinary hygiene; the passage is not advice to avoid washing.

**Editorial check (do not print):** This is a genuine grammatical petition; suitable as a short recitation from the full verse.

### No lamp is needed by the self-luminous  (`mpa-17b`)

## Social

### Speech as an offering  (`bhair-7`)
**Source:** Kṣemarāja, Bhairavānukaraṇastotra 7.
**Recitation:** FULL / NOT A POCKET MANTRA.
**Pocket words:** None proposed; retain the complete verse.
**Sense:** The poet describes speech as a Goddess and sense-objects as flowers of worship, followed by bowing and entering the heart.
**When:** Before a difficult conversation.
**What to do:** Pause for one natural breath and read or recall the verse. Listen to the other person before deciding what to say.
**Devotional recognition:** Treat words as something offered, and therefore worthy of care.
**Return:** Speak plainly rather than trying to sound mystical.
**Editorial check (do not print):** Author Kṣemarāja, not Abhinavagupta; local L2 source omitted viṣayakusumakḷptārcaḥ although full text includes it.

### Enough of difference-talk  (`mpa-8`)
**Source:** Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā 8.
**Recitation:** POCKET + FULL.
**Pocket words:** *alaṃ bhedānukathayā* — candidate excerpt; source-check before printing.
**Sense:** Enough of repeated discourse on distinctions. In the whole verse, the savour of devotion renders even the declaration “all is one” inadequate.
**When:** When a discussion becomes performative, especially a spiritual argument.
**What to do:** Say the short phrase inwardly once. Listen and consider whether another explanation is useful.
**Devotional recognition:** Nonduality is not another argument that must be won.
**Return:** Respond accurately if needed—or let the point rest.
**Editorial check (do not print):** Do not turn this into a ban on critical inquiry or difficult conversations.

## Food

### The meal is an offering  (`gita-424`)
**Source:** Bhagavad Gītā 4.24; Lakshmanjoo meal teaching.
**Recitation:** EXTRACT + FULL.
**Pocket words:** *brahmārpaṇaṃ brahma havir* — candidate excerpt; source-check before printing.
**Sense:** Offering-instrument, oblation, sacrificial fire, offerer and goal are all said to be Brahman. Lakshmanjoo reads this through Śiva.
**When:** Before the first bite.
**What to do:** Set down utensils. If following the taught custom, place a little clean water in the palm, recite the full verse, then eat. The short line is an optional memory cue, not a replacement prescribed by the Gītā.
**Devotional recognition:** Eating becomes an occasion to notice giver, gift and recipient in one field of awareness.
**Return:** Eat normally, gratefully and attentively.
**Editorial check (do not print):** Identify Gītā original vs Abhinavagupta’s Gītā commentary vs Lakshmanjoo’s teaching. Water-in-palm practice belongs to a particular teaching, not the bare verse.

### Śiva bows to Śiva  (`somananda`)
**Source:** Somānanda, Śivadṛṣṭi 1.1.
**Recitation:** HALF-VERSE + REF CORRECTION.
**Pocket words:** *śivaḥ karotu nijayā namaḥ śaktyā tatātmane* — candidate excerpt; source-check before printing.
**Sense:** The author asks Śiva, present as one’s own form and veiling himself through his own nature, to make an offering of homage to himself through his own power.
**When:** Before a meal, particularly if it follows Brahmārpaṇam.
**What to do:** Read or say the complete verse. Do not turn it into a ritual to erase guilt on command.
**Devotional recognition:** The actor, the impediment and the addressed Lord are contemplated within Śiva’s self-relation.
**Return:** Eat or return to the person sharing the meal.
**Editorial check (do not print):** Use 1.1, not 7.103; fix padaccheda according to checked edition; the pocket is the closing half, not a standalone attested mantra.

# The day, complete

You do not need to perform thirty-six prayers every day. Choose one that fits your life, know its words accurately, and practise it with attention. At waking, the room is present. At the sink, water falls. At work, a task asks for care. At the meal, food is received. In difficulty, devotion does not have to wait for a better mood. At night, the day can be released.

A good prayer makes the next action more truthful, not more theatrical. What has been offered returns to the world as ordinary responsibility.
