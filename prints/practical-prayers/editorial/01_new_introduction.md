# PRACTICAL PRAYERS
### Thirty-six ways to turn the ordinary day toward Śiva

A prayer is not a performance that must produce warmth, visions or a particular mood. It is an address, an offering, or a change in how an everyday action is understood. This book chooses passages from the Sanskrit nondual Śaiva tradition and asks one practical question of each: *what can I actually do with this, here and now?*

The selected texts do not all prescribe a domestic ritual. A hymn of praise, a philosophical assertion and a formal meditation instruction are different literary acts. Where a traditional action is documented, we say so. Where this book suggests a modern adaptation—pausing in a queue, looking at a window, setting down cutlery—we call it an adaptation, not a command from the ancient author.

**Two sizes, one source.** Every selection retains the complete Sanskrit passage with an exact reference once editorially verified. A **pocket phrase** appears only when the words are a meaningful contiguous extract; it is explicitly labelled an extract, not passed off as an independent mantra. Some verses are simply too rich to shorten. In those cases, read or listen to the whole piece and memorise it gradually—or do not memorise it at all.

For each prayer, first discover the words. Then find the action: a bow, an offering, a question, a moment of attention, a return to ordinary responsibility. The practice succeeds in its modest sense when it has become an honest act of devotion. No feeling needs to arrive on schedule.

The literary texts, the commentary tradition, Swami Lakshmanjoo's interpretations and the editor's practical suggestions are separately credited. Nondual interpretation does not erase grammar, historical source distinctions or the needs of an ordinary human body.
