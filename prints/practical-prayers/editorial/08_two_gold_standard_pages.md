# Two gold-standard prayer explanations

Sanskrit is quoted here for critical internal editorial review only; the shipping edition must derive the text from the edition-witness gate and ensure rights clearance. The ritual suggestions are ours, except where explicitly attributed.

## 1. Before eating — the meal as an offering

**Bhagavad Gītā 4.24**. This is Gītā Sanskrit interpreted in the Trika tradition, **not an original verse composed by Abhinavagupta**.

### Complete prayer

ब्रह्मार्पणं ब्रह्म हविर्ब्रह्माग्नौ ब्रह्मणा हुतम् ।
ब्रह्मैव तेन गन्तव्यं ब्रह्मकर्मसमाधिना ॥

*brahmārpaṇaṃ brahma havir brahmāgnau brahmaṇā hutam ।*
*brahmaiva tena gantavyaṃ brahmakarmasamādhinā ॥*

**Memory handle (EXTRACT, not a separate mantra):** *brahmārpaṇaṃ brahma havir* — “Brahman is the offering-implement; Brahman the oblation.”

### The words actually say

- **brahma / brahman**: Brahman, the absolute. Repeated across grammatical roles.
- **arpaṇam**: the act or means of offering; in this context a sacrificial implement/means. *brahma + arpaṇam → brahmārpaṇam* (a + a → ā), with an interpretive decision about the compound.
- **havis → haviḥ → havir**: the sacrificial oblation; the final -s/-ḥ changes to -r before the following voiced sound.
- **agnau**: locative singular of *agni*, “in the fire”; *brahma + agnau → brahmāgnau*.
- **brahmaṇā**: instrumental singular, “by Brahman.”
- **hutam**: past passive participle from √*hu*, “offered.”
- **brahma + eva → brahmaiva**: “Brahman alone/indeed.”
- **gantavyam**: gerundive from √*gam*, “to be reached.”
- **brahmakarmasamādhinā**: instrumental singular of a compound describing the person whose absorption concerns action as Brahman. A full derivation belongs in the companion grammar layer.

**Literal reading:** “The offering-implement is Brahman; Brahman is the oblation. It is offered by Brahman in the fire of Brahman. Brahman alone is what is to be reached by the one absorbed in action as Brahman.”

### The devotional act

**When:** Before the first bite.

**Say:** Recite the complete verse once if you know it; otherwise listen/read and learn it gradually.

**Do:** Set down the cutlery for a moment. Swami Lakshmanjoo taught a mealtime practice involving a little water in the hands; if following that particular custom, take clean water in the palms, then say the verse. This is the **teacher's reported practice**, not a gesture specified by Gītā 4.24 itself.

**Recognise:** The eating, food, fire of digestion, eater and offering are contemplated as expressions of one reality. Lakshmanjoo construes Brahman as Śiva in his explanation; the grammatical text is still the Gītā's Brahman.

**Return:** Eat normally and attentively. There is no required bodily heat, tingle, altered consciousness or ecstatic sign.

**Textual honesty:** Distinguish the Gītā, Abhinavagupta's Gītā interpretation, and Lakshmanjoo's application. Quote none of their prose as though it were an original verse; verify all actual commentary citations.

## 2. When caught in arguments — enough difference-talk

**Abhinavagupta, Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā 8.**

### Complete verse

अलं भेदानुकथया त्वद्भक्तिरसचर्वणात् ।
सर्वमेकमिदं शान्तमिति वक्तुं च लज्जते ॥

*alaṃ bhedānukathayā tvadbhaktirasacarvaṇāt ।*
*sarvam ekam idaṃ śāntam iti vaktuṃ ca lajjate ॥*

**Pocket line (EXTRACT):** **अलं भेदानुकथया — *alaṃ bhedānukathayā***.

### The words actually say

- **alaṃ**: “enough”; with an instrumental, “enough of ...”.
- **bheda**: difference or division, from √*bhid*, “to split.”
- **anukathā**: recounting or discussion; *anu-* here means “following/along,” not *aṇu*, “minute/limited individual.”
- **bheda + anukathā → bhedānukathā**: a + a → ā.
- **bhedānukathayā**: instrumental singular feminine, “with repeated discussion of difference.”
- **tvat-bhakti-rasa-carvaṇāt**: from savouring (*carvaṇa*) the flavour (*rasa*) of devotion (*bhakti*) to you (*tvat*); final -āt is ablative singular.
- **sarvam ekam idaṃ śāntam**: “all this is one, at peace”; *sarvam, ekam, idam, śāntam* share neuter singular form in the proposition being quoted.
- **iti**: closes the proposition being quoted; **vaktum**: infinitive “to say”; **lajjate**: third-person singular middle present of √*lajj*, “feels ashamed/shrinks from saying.”

**Literal reading:** “Enough of the continual discussion of difference! From the savour of the flavour of devotion to You, one is ashamed even to say, ‘All this is one, peaceful.’”

### The devotional act

**When:** When explaining nonduality has become an argument or a performance.

**Say:** One recitation of the short phrase is sufficient; it is a clearly labelled excerpt, not an independent mantra transmitted by the author.

**Do:** Stop for a moment. Notice that the situation includes the other person, your own urge to win, and the capacity to listen. No special breath technique is needed.

**Recognise:** Abhinavagupta is not announcing that truth is inaccessible to reasoning. The stanza contrasts discursive assertion with the immediate savour of devotion. Even “everything is one” becomes an inadequate slogan if used in place of recognition.

**Return:** If something needs to be said, say it carefully. Otherwise, allow the conversation to end.

**Textual honesty:** The Sanskrit phrase has an intelligible grammatical use as a concise reminder, but the practice of repeating it during arguments is **our modern adaptation**, not an instruction stated in the stanza.
