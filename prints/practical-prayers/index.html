Why these prayers Swami Lakshmanjoo compiled his Wisdom verses in the summer of 1987 and told his devotees: "These verses are for your daily recitation. Maybe sometime you will experience these stages." Four of the six hymns in his recitation appendix live in these pages — the Krama Stotra, the Anuttarāṣṭikā, the Bhairava Stotra and the Dehastha — alongside the mealtime yajña he taught, the breath-japa of the Vijñāna Bhairava, and the dawn-dusk junction of the Tantrāloka: "the Lord must be worshipped at the point of the meeting of day and night." Nine sections — eight moments of the day, plus one for any time. Thirty-six prayers, each facing its explanation — when to do it, how, why, and what it does. Three of Swamiji's Bahurūpa hymns live in the Anytime pocket. Nothing here needs belief. The verses were built to be said — at the sink, at the desk, in the queue, in the dark.
Practical Prayers

Decoding the verses — the axioms (1 of 2)

Śiva — consciousness that is self-lit: knowing that needs no second thing in order to be known. Why: any knower requiring another knower regresses forever; the chain of proof must end in something self-evidencing. That self-evidencing is what the texts name Śiva. (svaprakāśa)

Śakti — Śiva knowing himself: power not as muscle but as the act of shining. Why: "self-lit" means lit by itself — that "by" is Śakti. Flame and burning are one word's two halves.

Brahman (in the meal verse) — read as Śiva in all four places: offering, oblation, fire, goal. Why: Gītā 4.24 names them all "brahman"; if any one differed, the chain would break into parts — one substance makes the meal a self-offering. (Lakshmanjoo, mealtime teaching)

So'ham / haṃsa — the mantra already running: out sa, in ha. Why: Vijñāna Bhairava 155 — the jīva recites it 21,600 times a day without trying; so real practice adds no sound, it only shortens the gap (Swamiji's tuṭi-method) until you notice it.

Pañcākṣara — naḥ·maḥ·śi·vā·ya = the five acts: creation, preservation, dissolution, concealment, grace. Why: nothing in experience falls outside those five — so five sounds cover the whole cycle, which is why one mantra can be the entire practice.

Anātha — the refuge that asks for no qualifications. Why: a protector requiring worthy applicants is a merchant; "refuge of the refugeless" (Bhairavastava 1) removes the condition — grace that cannot be refused.

Decoding the verses — the axioms (2 of 2)

Anupāya — the no-means. Why: if every method — contemplation, japa, visualization — can be dropped and recognition still arrives, method was never the cause. Anuttarāṣṭikā 1 simply lists what must be set down.

Pratyabhijñā — recognition, not achievement: you don't come to know Śiva; you notice you never stopped. Why: bondage must be real for liberation to be sought — yet if it were real, no freedom could ever be felt; you do feel the difference, so bondage is a misreading, and what corrects a misreading is recognition.

Svātantrya — freedom: the cause that needs no cause. Why: every chain of causation stops somewhere or loops; the texts place the stop at freedom — the one that acts without being acted on.

Pūjā — offering as re-cognition of what is already there. Why: the litany (Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā 13–20) proves the Lord needs nothing from the rite — what the rite changes is the offerer. The worship is the worshipper, turning.

Bhakti — the method that is not a method. Why: Śivastotrāvalī 1 describes a devotee attained without meditation or recitation — devotion works by alignment, not technique; that is why these guides give feelings, not repetitions.

Māyā — the world's making-power, seen from underneath. Why: the texts never deny the world — "the world, both real and unreal" (Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā 11) — they deny only the view from below that takes the making for the made.

The Secret Remedy

शृणु देवी! परं गुह्यं रहस्यं परमाद्भुतम् । सर्व-पाप-प्रशमनं सर्व-दुःख-निवारणम् ॥ प्रायश्चित्तेषु सर्वेषु तीव्रेष्व् अपि विमोचनम् । सर्व-च्छिद्राप-हरणं सर्वार्ति-विनिवारकम् ॥ समयोलङ्घने घोरे जपादेव विमोचनम् । भोग-मोक्ष्ह-प्रदं देवी! सर्व-सिद्धि-फलप्रदम् ॥
śṛṇu devī! paraṁ guhyaṁ rahasyaṁ paramādbhutam | sarva-pāpa-praśamanaṁ sarva-duḥkha-nivāraṇam || prāyaścitteṣu sarveṣu tīvreṣv api vimocanam | sarva-cchidrāpa-haraṇaṁ sarvārti-vinivārakam || samayolaṅghane ghore japādeva vimocanam | bhoga-mokṣha-pradaṁ devī! sarva-siddhi-phalapradam ||
listen, goddess: the supreme secret, most wonderful — it pacifies all sins, removes all sorrows; releases even from intense atonement, removes every gap, uproots all affliction; in the breaking of the vows the remedy is the recitation alone — it grants enjoyment and liberation, goddess, the fruits of all accomplishments.

The Secret Remedy

Śrī Bahurūpa-Garbha Stotra, Bhairava's answer (vv.5-7) · vv.5-7 · Academy recitation PDF
The Devī asks for the easy remedy; Bhairava answers: the recitation itself.
What is it

The hymn's frame: Bhairavī approaches Svacchanda Bhairava on Kailāśa and asks for the upāya for those who have broken the rules, however sincerely they try to atone. Bhairava's answer is not a ritual but a text — japādeva vimocanam, liberation by the recitation itself. It purifies the three channels, closes the chidras (the flaws and gaps in conduct and rite), and grants both bhoga and mokṣa. The logic of a remedy-hymn: the mantra is the medicine, and the disease is as much guilt as transgression.

How to perform it

Recite it at the beginning and end of any ritual — the hymn's own instruction (yāgārambhe ca yāgānte). Also daily (nitya), on occasion (naimitika), when you want something (kāmya), for yourself or for someone else. Counting effects per the hymn: one hundred recitations purify even great sinners; fifty, inadvertent sins; twenty-five, minor sins — and a single recitation (of this hymn or of the Aghora japa) removes wrongs done by body, speech or mind, whether through inattention or knowingly.

How to know it's working

a stuck state — anxiety, craving, the low hum — is named as him and loses its stuckness; the secret shows as the shift.

What to feel / visualise

the ease is the point — sadupāyaṁ, the remedy without strain.

No Mantra Greater

Śrī Bahurūpa-Garbha Stotra, the close (vv.13-15) · vv.13-15 · viniyoga
The hymn's own claim: no mantra greater, no counter-formula better.
What is it

Three closing claims: (1) it covers every action, including mistakes of omission and negligence (pramāda); (2) it is samyak-pratyaṅgirā — the perfect counter-charm against ritual flaw — the queen of samaya-vidyā, the knowledge of the precepts; (3) its effect is āpyāyana, nourishment — of Bhairava's glorification, pleasing to all the gods, increasing saubhāgya, all good fortune. The hymn then gives its own viniyoga: Ṛṣi Vāmadeva, meter Anuṣṭupa, devatā Bahurūpabhaṭṭāraka, purpose — removal of sins of body, speech and mind, and attainment of the four goals: dharma, artha, kāma, mokṣa.

How to perform it

Full practice in the tradition: dhyāna of Svacchanda (crystal-white upward face, yellow east, blue south, pomegranate-red north, moon-white west — the twelve-verse meditation), then this hymn, then the Gāyatrī — oṁ bahurupāya vidmahe, koṭarākṣāya dhīmahi, tanno 'ghoraḥ pracodayāt, three times — then the mūla mantra. In this book's simpler use: one round of these three verses at the close of any difficult day; the line to remember is 'no mantra greater than this'.

How to know it's working

the tongue stops wanting to stop: the mantra runs during ordinary tasks, unsummoned — addiction-to-the-name.

The Guide

Bahurūpa Gāyatrī

Śrī Bahurūpa-Garbha Stotra — the gāyatrī · gāyatrī · viniyoga in recitation PDF
The hymn's Gāyatrī — three tenses of prayer in three lines.
What is it

The classical Gāyatrī frame bent onto Bahurūpabhaṭṭāraka: vidmahe (we know), dhīmahi (we dwell), pracodayāt (may he impel) — knowing, dwelling, directing. Bahurūpa = manifold-formed: forms plural, power one. Koṭarākṣa, hundred-thousand-eyed, answers the dhyāna's fifteen eyes — the seeing multiplied.

How to perform it

The recitation order per the viniyoga: dhyāna → hymn → this gāyatrī three times → mūla mantra. Three rounds, seed-voice. 'Pracodayāt' is the only request in the entire sequence — everything before it was arrangement.

How to know it's working

brow, chest and navel warm in sequence on the rounds — and the day bends toward him three times without effort.

Oṁ Namaḥ Śivāya

Pañcākṣara mantra · practice file
The most portable Trika prayer: the whole cycle of manifestation folded into sound.
What is it

The five syllables are the five acts: naḥ creation, maḥ preservation, śi dissolution, vā concealment, ya grace. Oṁ, a+u+ṃ, is the triad of will, knowledge and action. To repeat it is to let the whole cycle turn inside you.

How to perform it

Say it aloud or mentally: let naḥ open, maḥ hold, śi release, vā hide, ya return. The Name does the work — no count needed, pick it up any hour.

How to know it's working

The practice file's own feel track: heaviness lifting to warmth, then offering-release, luminosity, pervasion — the cycle noticed turning without your steering.

What Bath for the Stainless?

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.14 · v.14
Shower, towel, scent, jewel — all addressed to one who needs none.
What is it

The litany names the bathroom itself: snāna (bath), vastra (garment), gandha (scent), bharaṇa (jewel) — each with a deity stripped of the lack it serves: stainless, world-bellied (all-containing), unanointed, lovely. What looks like futility is the precise applicability of each gift: you bathe the unblemished because bathing is what love does, not what dirt requires.

How to perform it

In the shower: name each item as you use it — water for the stainless, towel for the world-bellied, scent for the unanointed. The mismatch is the smile.

How to know it's working

the shower turns comic-sacred: water for the stainless lands as gift, not chore — three items, three small bows, the water felt offered.

What to feel / visualise

the water becomes an offering rather than a chore; three items, three small bows.

This Body for Worship

What is it

Waving-Lights for the Self-Shining

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.17 · v.17
The mirror, the lamp: he lights himself — so the light is for you.
What is it

Two paradoxes: an offering of light presupposes darkness in the recipient; speech presupposes an object speech can reach. The Lord is self-lit and beyond the Veda's words. The rite's use falls on the offerer — you wave the lamp so your own eyes open; you sing because the singing lights you.

How to perform it

At the bathroom mirror, hand raised with the light: you are the lamp-bearer, and the face that brightens is the audience. Hum, don't articulate — you have just honoured the Vedavācāmavedya.

How to know it's working

the mirror-wave brightens chest-first; the lamp-waving happens inward, and muttering becomes humming.

What to feel / visualise

the chest brightens before the mirror does.

Abandon Nothing, Grasp Nothing

Abhinavagupta, Anuttarāṣṭikā v.2 · anu-p02 v.2
The waiting instruction par excellence: don't add, don't subtract, just stay.
What is it

The verse dismantles bondage itself — saṃsāra never truly was; the bondage-story is the rope-snake-shadow ghost of māyā. Then the instruction in four beats: mā kiṃcit tyaja (abandon nothing), mā gṛhāṇa (grasp nothing), vihara (live), svastho yathāvasthitaḥ (stay as you are). the hands open and close once, then rest; the waiting is now the living.

No Fear-Talk

From Your Waking, His Waking

Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā v.11 · v.11

Two transfers: prabodha (waking) passes from you to him, laya (dissolution) from his sleep to the world. as the eyes close, feel the day's edges get soft, 'unreal' — that softening is sadasadātmakam. If anxious at night, this dissolves the day rather than reviewing it.

In Sleep, When the Outer Range Perished

Vijñāna Bhairava v.75 · v.75
The instruction for the moment before sleep: go there by mind.
What is it

Three conditions: sleep that has not yet arrived; once the outer field has died. In that gap, to be gone to by mind, parā devī, the supreme Goddess, shines — not is-made.
