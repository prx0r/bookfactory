## 1. The Poetry of Abhinavagupta
**Subtitle:** Nine hymns of recognition · thirty-six verses
**Thesis (draft):** the philosopher everyone quotes as prose was first a poet
of recognition — his hymns carry the same doctrine in the register of address,
and address is the part a reader can actually enter.

### Reader promise
The mainstream Abhinavagupta: the famous, anthologised, singable verses — not
the 37 chapters. One page of verse, one page of orientation, no Sanskrit
required.

### Structure — 9 sections × 4 verses (one section per hymn)
| Section | Hymn | Corpus verses | Gold |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Bhairavastava | 10 | ✓ |
| II | Dehasthadevatācakra | 16 | ✓ |
| III | Kramastotra | 30 | ✓ |
| IV | Anuttarāṣṭikā | 8 | ✓ |
| V | Paramārthadvādaśikā | 13 | ✓ |
| VI | Mahopadeśaviṃśatikā | 20 | ✓ |
| VII | Paryantapañcāśikā | 52 | ✓ |
| VIII | Bodhapañcadaśikā | 15 | ✓ |
| IX | Paramārthacarcā | 8 | ✓ |

Only 4 verses per hymn are taken here; **the rest are reserved for the deep
volumes** (see series plan) so nothing ships twice.

### Entry anatomy (poetry variant of the prayer-book spread)
left: Devanagari + IAST + translation · right: **What it praises · How to
hear it · What it does · What to feel**. The guide blocks are reading notes,
not practice instructions — this is the one template-label change.

### Page budget

10 · end matter 8 = **108**.

reappear.

### Risk

job. Emblem: trident-lotus (series constant).

## 2. Recognition: A Manual
**Subtitle:** Why you want it · how to do it · how you know
**Thesis (draft):** recognition is not an experience to be produced but a fact
to be noticed; this is the shortest rigorous path from the ordinary sense of
being a separate person to the recognition of oneself as the whole, with the
texts' own practices and their own tests.

### Reader promise
A front-door book: terse, practical, unsentimental. It answers the three
questions a smart sceptic actually asks — *why would I want this, what exactly
is being recognised, and how do I know it happened* — without devotional
padding or invented prose.

### Structure — 9 sections × 4 entries (a curriculum, in order)
| Section | Movement | The four entries answer |
|---|---|---|
| I | **The Knot** | why the ordinary self feels incomplete; what misidentification costs |
| II | **The Given** | what is always already present in experience |
| III | **The Subject** | you are not the body, the mind, or the thought |
| IV | **The Object** | the world is not outside awareness (without solipsism) |
| V | **The Other** | recognition is relational, not private |
| VI | **The Energies** | desire, knowing, action — icchā · jñāna · kriyā |
| VII | **The Methods** | the actual practices, placed from the texts |
| VIII | **The Signs** | how you know it is working |
| IX | **The Ordinary** | post-recognition life: nothing special, everything changed |

### Entry anatomy (four faces — the "why/what/how/sign" contract)
- **Cost** — what the unrecognised view costs (from C1 essence)
- **Recognition** — the precise seeing (from C1 essence + L2)
- **Practice** — the text's own method (from `how` blocks; placed, never written)
- **Sign** — the text's own claimed effect (from `sign`)

No agent-written prose. The 36 questions are editorial structure (owner-ruled,
like section ids); every answer is placed from the gold layers.

### Source partition (respecting one-verse-one-book)
Draws only from verses NOT assigned elsewhere:
- Spanda (25, gold) — the core of The Given / The Subject
- Tantrasāra gold āhnikas — the methods and the energies
- Unused VBT verses (126 of 162 remain after *Book of Doors*)
- Unused Śiva Sūtra verses (none remain — *Aphorisms* takes all 75)
- Pratyabhijñā argument verses NOT in *Proofs of the Self* (the siddhis are
  fully consumed by book 4)
- Parātrīśikā verses NOT in the goddess book (all 36 are consumed there)

So the manual's spine is **Spanda + Tantrasāra + leftover VBT** — a real
curricular line, not a greatest-hits reprint.

### Page budget
36 × 2 = 72 · 18 dividers/ornaments · front 10 · end 8 = **108**.

### Risk
Medium: curation and tone. It must stay terse; the temptation is a treatise.
This is the book that sells the other five, so it ships early.

## 3. The Seventy-Five Aphorisms of Śiva
**Subtitle:** Śiva Sūtra, one sutra at a time
**Thesis (draft):** the shortest complete scripture in the tradition is
seventy-five sentences; read one at a time, each is a door, and the whole
fits in the hand.

### Reader promise
The gift book: one sutra, one page, one clean sentence of orientation. The
most quotable object the imprint can make.

### Structure — the text's own order, 9 readings summing to 75
Chapters follow the Śiva Sūtra's own movement (three unmeṣas read as nine
thematic readings). **Exact verse counts are read from the corpus, not
assumed:** the proposed cut is 9/9/9/9/9/8/8/7/7 = 75.

Proposed readings (mapped against the actual 75 verses, owner-ruled):
I. Consciousness is the self · II. Knowledge is bondage · III. The will ·
IV. The word · V. The body · VI. The powers · VII. The world ·
VIII. The means · IX. The recognition.

### Layout — new `aphorism` entry
One page per sutra:
```
               1.21
        चित्तं मन्त्रः
        cittaṃ mantraḥ
        Mind is mantra.
        ─────────────
        two-to-four line orientation (from C1 essence)
```
No facing page. The page is the object.

### Template work required
- New template `aphorism-book` (`templates/aphorism-book.json`).
- `structure.sections_plan[].count` (variable per section; today the schema
  assumes one integer `entries_per_section`).
- Engine `layout: "aphorism"` (single-page entry, verse top, gloss below).
- Gate: sum of section counts = total entries; one-page layout implies
  pages = entries + dividers, checked by preflight.

### Page budget
75 sutra pages · 9 recto openers + 9 ornament versos = 18 · front 10 ·
end 5 = **108**.

### Risk
Medium (build, not content — source is complete gold). The glosses are C1
one-liners, not new commentary.

## 4. Proofs of the Self — The Three Siddhis
**Subtitle:** Ajadapramātṛsiddhi · Īśvarasiddhi · Sambandhasiddhi
**Thesis (draft):** recognition is not a belief to be adopted but a conclusion
to be earned; three short treatises prove the subject is not inert, the agent
is universal, and the relation between the two is real.

### First, the honest answer: is it a proof of God?
**Partly — and that is the point.** The three treatises are not devotional
theism:
- **Ajadapramātṛsiddhi (27 v)** — the perceiver cannot be inert matter or an
  atomic point; the subject is conscious, self-luminous, non-objectifiable.
- **Īśvarasiddhi (56 v)** — there must be a universal conscious agent (the
  Lord) in which all subjects and objects cohere. *This is the "proof of God"
  third* — but the Lord proved is the universal subject, not a creator-king
  who intervenes.
- **Sambandhasiddhi (23 v)** — the relation between the universal agent and
  the individual subject is real, not an illusion; that relation is what
  recognition sees.

So the book's claim is: **recognition is rational**. One third is a proof of
the Lord, two thirds are a proof of you and of the relation between you —
which is why it is the intellectual spine of the whole imprint.

### Structure — 3 treatises × 3 movements = 9 chapters, all 106 verses
| Ch | Section | Verses | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | The Subject Is Not Inert | Ajad 1–9 | 9 |
| II | The Subject Is Self-Luminous | Ajad 10–18 | 9 |
| III | The Subject Is Free | Ajad 19–27 | 9 |
| IV | The World Requires a Knower | Īśvara 1–19 | 19 |
| V | The Knower Is One | Īśvara 20–38 | 19 |
| VI | The One Is the Lord | Īśvara 39–56 | 18 |
| VII | Relation Is Not an Illusion | Sambandha 1–8 | 8 |
| VIII | The Universal and the Individual | Sambandha 9–16 | 8 |
| IX | Recognition Is the Relation | Sambandha 17–23 | 7 |

Total 106. *(Section boundaries are proposed; the verse text rules.)*

### How 106 verses fit in 108 pages — the arithmetic
Do **not** use the prayer-book spread: 106 × 2 = 212 + matter, impossible.
Use the **treatise layout** (continuous, two verses per page):

| Block | Pages |
|---|---|
| front matter (half/title/copyright/epigraph/contents) | 10 |
| 9 chapter essays (1.5 pp each, from argmap + C1) | 14 |
| 106 verses @ 2 per page (Deva + IAST + English + 3-line note) | 53 |
| glossary of technical terms | 8 |
| index of verses / works | 6 |
| end matter (colophon, sources, notes) | 4 |
| **subtotal** | **95** |
| pad to 108 with notes/ornament versos | 13 |

At three verses per page the verse block drops to 36 pages and you gain room
for longer essays — that is the owner's density choice, not a gate question.
Either way the whole 106 fits; what does not fit is one-verse-per-spread.

### Template work required
- New template `treatise-book`.
- `layout: "treatise"`: continuous verse blocks (2/page), chapter essays,
  glossary, index.
- Variable section counts (shared with the aphorism template).
- A glossary is **placed**, not written: terms + C1 key-term definitions.

### Risk
Highest editorial (the nine essays must be C1/argmap condensations, not new
prose) and a real build (treatise layout + glossary + index). It is the
prestige book and should ship last.
