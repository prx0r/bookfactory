## Proposed introduction

You can recognise an error in a sentence without finding a new object. You can discover that the person you were looking for has been in the room all along. Recognition is different from producing the person by thinking correctly. Nondual Śaiva philosophy applies a radical version of this idea to awareness and the world. It argues that what you take to be a separate, insufficient self is an appearance within a more fundamental consciousness.

This is not an empirical fact you must accept before turning the page. The following thirty-six short inquiries ask you to notice distinctions that philosophy can use and to see where it must still argue. They cannot replace a lineage's initiation, prove every metaphysical claim, or guarantee a permanent state. The work begins with what you can check now: how a thought appears, what you mean by “I,” and whether what is known exhausts knowing itself.

Each page contains an ordinary cost, a question, a careful recognitional distinction, and an action that returns the inquiry to life. When a page makes a bigger metaphysical claim than its observation entails, the difference must remain visible. This is a manual for accurate attention, not a certificate of enlightenment.

## Knot

### 01. The wrong size [rec-01]
**The ordinary cost.** You feel too small for your life because you measure yourself against everything that happens.

**The inquiry.** What exactly is being measured: the body, a passing thought, or the awareness in which both are known?

**The precise turn.** Do not confuse the fact of being aware with an inflated claim that you are personally omnipotent

**Return.** When the next small task arrives, do it at its actual scale.

## Final page: a complete ordinary act

No final exercise asks you to manufacture a state. The inquiry has come through thoughts, objects, other people, action and the hazards of measuring progress. Each time it returned to something concrete. If there is a recognition worth keeping, it will have to survive that return. The door opens onto the same street. Nothing in the street is required to know that you have read a book.
