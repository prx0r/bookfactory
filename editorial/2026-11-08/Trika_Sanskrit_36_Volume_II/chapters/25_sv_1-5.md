# 25. Without a second, yet with You

**Stage 5: Devotion without falsifying the grammar**  
**Utpaladeva · *Śivastotrāvalī* 1.5**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

अद्वितीया अपि सदा त्वद्द्वितीया अपि प्रभो

*advitīyā api sadā tvaddvitīyā api prabho*

### The complete verse in context

जयन्ति भक्तिपीयूषरसासववरोन्मदाः । अद्वितीया अपि सदा त्वद्द्वितीया अपि प्रभो ॥

*jayanti bhaktipīyūṣarasāsavavaronmadāḥ | advitīyā api sadā tvaddvitīyā api prabho ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** द्वि includes द् + व; ती has long ī; तीयाः masculine plural becomes तिया before api under sandhi; प्रभो has long o.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **advitīyā** | a- + dvitīya | masculine nominative plural before vowel | without a second, nondual |
| **api** | api | indeclinable concessive | even though |
| **sadā** | sadā | indeclinable adverb | always |
| **tvaddvitīyā** | tvad + dvitīya | masculine nominative plural | having You as their second |
| **prabho** | prabhu | masculine vocative singular | O Lord |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **advitīyā** = without a second, nondual · **api** = even though · **sadā** = always · **tvaddvitīyā** = having You as their second · **prabho** = O Lord.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. a- + dvitīya → advitīya (not possessing a second)
2. advitīyāḥ + api → advitīyā api (visarga of masculine āḥ is lost before initial a)
3. tvad + dvitīya makes tvaddvitīya with assimilated consonant cluster
4. prabhu vocative singular is prabho

**What the derivation demonstrates.** An unforgettable compound paradox whose grammar prevents a superficial “nonduality means no love” conclusion.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** Though without a second, they always have You as their second, O Lord.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** Even in nonduality, devotees remain in relationship to You.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** The apparent contradiction is deliberately poetic. Advitīya is not simply “lonely”; tvaddvitīya is a relational compound, not a literal assertion that reality has become numerically two.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

Utpaladeva keeps both the address to the Lord and the recognition of nonduality. In a living devotional idiom, relation need not mean separation: the second person survives as a form of intimacy.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
