# 12. He makes creation and withdrawal

**Stage 2: From naming to doing**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Bodhapañcadaśikā* 6b**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

विचित्रान् सृष्टिसंहारान् विधत्ते युगपद् विभुः

*vicitrān sṛṣṭisaṃhārān vidhatte yugapad vibhuḥ*

### The complete verse in context

एष देवोऽनया देव्या नित्यं क्रीडारसोत्सुकः । विचित्रान् सृष्टिसंहारान् विधत्ते युगपद् विभुः ॥

*eṣa devo 'nayā devyā nityaṃ krīḍārasotsukaḥ | vicitrān sṛṣṭisaṃhārān vidhatte yugapad vibhuḥ ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** सृष्टि has vocalic ṛ sign ृ and conjunct ष्ट; संहार uses anusvāra; वि-ध-त्ते contains त्त.

### All sounds, one reading unit at a time

| IAST word | Devanāgarī word | Reading units (IAST = script) |
|---|---|---|
| *vicitrān* | विचित्रान् | vi = वि · ci = चि · trā = त्रा · n = न् |
| *sṛṣṭisaṃhārān* | सृष्टिसंहारान् | sṛ = सृ · ṣṭi = ष्टि · saṃ = सं · hā = हा · rā = रा · n = न् |
| *vidhatte* | विधत्ते | vi = वि · dha = ध · tte = त्ते |
| *yugapad* | युगपद् | yu = यु · ga = ग · pa = प · d = द् |
| *vibhuḥ* | विभुः | vi = वि · bhuḥ = भुः |

These are **orthographic reading units** to help you construct Devanāgarī, not a metrical scansion. When a consonant ends a word without an inherent vowel, notice its virāma. Trace the conjuncts rather than inserting English-style epenthetic vowels.

Write the selected IAST in Devanāgarī using the reference sheet. Check consonants, vowel lengths and the presence of anusvāra (ं), visarga (ः) and virāma (्). It is fine to consult the chart; the point is a repeatable skill, not instant recognition.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **vicitrān** | vicitra | masculine accusative plural | varied, manifold |
| **sṛṣṭisaṃhārān** | sṛṣṭi + saṃhāra | masculine accusative plural compound | creations and dissolutions / manifestations and reabsorptions |
| **vidhatte** | vi-√dhā | present middle third singular | brings about, performs, arranges |
| **yugapad** | yugapat | indeclinable | simultaneously, at once |
| **vibhuḥ** | vibhu | masculine nominative singular | all-pervading lord |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **vicitrān** = varied, manifold · **sṛṣṭisaṃhārān** = creations and dissolutions · **vidhatte** = brings about, performs, arranges · **yugapad** = simultaneously, at once · **vibhuḥ** = all-pervading lord.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. vicitra + -ān → vicitrān
2. sṛṣṭi + saṃhāra → sṛṣṭisaṃhāra, accusative plural -ān
3. vi + √dhā gives vidhatte in middle voice (do not derive by mere external sandhi)
4. yugapat → yugapad before voiced v

**What the derivation demonstrates.** First nontrivial full verbal sentence: agreement and object marking become unmistakable.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** The all-pervading one simultaneously brings about varied creations and reabsorptions.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** The omnipresent Lord fashions manifold unfoldings and withdrawals all at once.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** Sṛṣṭi/saṃhāra are paired cosmological actions; a literal reading does not require an astronomical claim that historical universes physically appear simultaneously.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

After 11’s “play” this line gives its verbs and objects. The grammar makes clear what he does (accusative plural), how (yugapad), and who does it (vibhuḥ).

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
