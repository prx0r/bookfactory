# 22. When intelligence becomes clear

**Stage 4: The world in the sentence**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Paramārthasāra* 9**

## The Sanskrit

शिवशक्तिपातविमले धीतत्त्वे भाति भारूपः

*śivaśaktipātavimale dhītattve bhāti bhārūpaḥ*

### The complete verse in context

आदर्शे मलरहिते यद्वद् वदनं विभाति तद्वद् अयम् । शिवशक्तिपातविमले धीतत्त्वे भाति भारूपः ॥

*ādarśe malarahite yadvad vadanaṃ vibhāti tadvad ayam | śivaśaktipātavimale dhītattve bhāti bhārūpaḥ ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** शक्तिपात has क्ति conjunct and long ā in पात; धी long ī; भाति has long ā.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **śivaśaktipātavimale** | śiva + śakti + pāta + vimala | neuter locative singular compound adjective | made clear by the descent of Śiva’s power |
| **dhītattve** | dhī + tattva | neuter locative singular compound | in the principle of intellect |
| **bhāti** | √bhā | present active third singular | shines, appears |
| **bhārūpaḥ** | bhā + rūpa | masculine nominative singular adjective | light-formed, of the nature of luminosity |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **śivaśaktipātavimale** = made clear by the descent of Śiva’s power · **dhītattve** = in the principle of intellect · **bhāti** = shines, appears · **bhārūpaḥ** = light-formed, of the nature of luminosity.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. śiva + śaktipāta makes “Śiva’s grace/descent of power”
2. vimala + locative -e → vimale
3. dhī + tattva + locative -e → dhītattve
4. √bhā + present -ti → bhāti
5. bhā + rūpa → bhārūpaḥ

**What the derivation demonstrates.** Explains how context supplies a subject across verses and resists experiential sensationalism.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** In the principle of intellect purified by the descent of Śiva’s power, the luminous one shines.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** Where the intellect is clear through Śiva’s śaktipāta, the Self appears as light itself.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** The masculine subject “the luminous one” is linked to ayam ātmā in the surrounding passage. “Light” is a metaphor for self-disclosure, not a prediction of visible photic phenomena.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

The two locatives describe the place or cognitive condition of appearance. The poem does not assert that one should strain to produce a light vision.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
