# 18. The grammar of liberation

**Stage 3: What does Śakti actually do?**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Bodhapañcadaśikā* 13a**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

यत् तत्त्वस्य परिज्ञानं स मोक्षः परमेशतः

*yat tattvasya parijñānaṃ sa mokṣaḥ parameśataḥ*

### The complete verse in context

यत् तत्त्वस्य परिज्ञानं स मोक्षः परमेशतः । तत् पूर्णत्वं प्रबुद्धानां जीवन्मुक्तिश् च सा स्मृता ॥

*yat tattvasya parijñānaṃ sa mokṣaḥ parameśataḥ | tat pūrṇatvaṃ prabuddhānāṃ jīvanmuktiś ca sā smṛtā ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** तत्त्व is त + त्त्व; मोक्ष includes क्ष = क् + ष; परिज्ञान has ज्ञ.

### All sounds, one reading unit at a time

| IAST word | Devanāgarī word | Reading units (IAST = script) |
|---|---|---|
| *yat* | यत् | ya = य · t = त् |
| *tattvasya* | तत्त्वस्य | ta = त · ttva = त्त्व · sya = स्य |
| *parijñānaṃ* | परिज्ञानं | pa = प · ri = रि · jñā = ज्ञा · naṃ = नं |
| *sa* | स | sa = स |
| *mokṣaḥ* | मोक्षः | mo = मो · kṣaḥ = क्षः |
| *parameśataḥ* | परमेशतः | pa = प · ra = र · me = मे · śa = श · taḥ = तः |

These are **orthographic reading units** to help you construct Devanāgarī, not a metrical scansion. When a consonant ends a word without an inherent vowel, notice its virāma. Trace the conjuncts rather than inserting English-style epenthetic vowels.

Write the selected IAST in Devanāgarī using the reference sheet. Check consonants, vowel lengths and the presence of anusvāra (ं), visarga (ः) and virāma (्). It is fine to consult the chart; the point is a repeatable skill, not instant recognition.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **yat** | yad | neuter nominative relative pronoun | that which |
| **tattvasya** | tattva | neuter genitive singular | of reality / truth |
| **parijñānam** *(recovered)* | pari-√jñā + -ana | neuter nominative singular | complete knowing or recognition |
| **sa** | sa [from saḥ] | masculine nominative demonstrative | that |
| **mokṣaḥ** | mokṣa (√muc family) | masculine nominative singular | liberation |
| **parameśataḥ** | parameśa + -tas | ablatival adverbial formation | from / on account of the Supreme Lord |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **yat** = that which · **tattvasya** = of reality · **parijñānam** = complete knowing or recognition · **sa** = that · **mokṣaḥ** = liberation · **parameśataḥ** = from.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. pari + jñāna yields parijñāna (lexical formation)
2. tattva + -sya → tattvasya
3. saḥ + mokṣaḥ → sa mokṣaḥ (before nasal m)
4. parameśa + -tas → parameśataḥ, source or standpoint depending on construal

**What the derivation demonstrates.** A line that lets the student deduce “liberation” from Sanskrit syntax without relying on an English subtitle.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** Complete understanding of reality—this is liberation, through the Supreme Lord.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** What is recognition of the real is precisely what the text calls liberation.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** Note the change of gender: yat/parijñānam are neuter; saḥ/mokṣaḥ masculine. The anaphor can agree with its predicate noun while referring back to a neuter event. Do not invent a separate “he” who is liberated.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

This supplies the natural answer to lesson 17: ignorance and recognition are not simply feelings to arrange; the text gives a definition of liberation in terms of knowing.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
