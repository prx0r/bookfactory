# 16. Why awareness differs

**Stage 3: What does Śakti actually do?**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Bodhapañcadaśikā* 8b**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

जडाद् विलक्षणो बोधो यतो न परिमीयते

*jaḍād vilakṣaṇo bodho yato na parimīyate*

### The complete verse in context

परिच्छिन्नप्रकाशत्वं जडस्य किल लक्षणम् । जडाद् विलक्षणो बोधो यतो न परिमीयते ॥

*paricchinnaprakāśatvaṃ jaḍasya kila lakṣaṇam | jaḍād vilakṣaṇo bodho yato na parimīyate ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** जडात् is ablative; जडाद् is its voiced sandhi outcome. मीय in परिमीयते writes long ī.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **jaḍād** | jaḍa | ablative singular before voiced v | from / unlike the inert |
| **vilakṣaṇo** | vilakṣaṇa | masculine nominative singular | different in characteristic |
| **bodho** | bodha | masculine nominative singular | awareness, cognition |
| **yato** | yato [from yataḥ] | indeclinable causal relative/ablative-form | because, since |
| **na** | na | negation | not |
| **parimīyate** | pari-√mā | present passive 3rd singular | is measured, circumscribed |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **jaḍād** = from · **vilakṣaṇo** = different in characteristic · **bodho** = awareness, cognition · **yato** = because, since · **na** = not · **parimīyate** = is measured, circumscribed.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. jaḍāt + vilakṣaṇaḥ → jaḍād vilakṣaṇaḥ (t→d before voiced v)
2. vilakṣaṇaḥ + bodhaḥ → vilakṣaṇo bodhaḥ (-aḥ→-o)
3. bodhaḥ + yataḥ → bodho yataḥ
4. yataḥ + na → yato na
5. pari-√mā passive “is measured” → parimīyate

**What the derivation demonstrates.** Our first explicit premise-and-contrast argument, with three exact consonant/visarga transformations.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** Awareness differs from the inert, because it is not measurable or bounded.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** Conscious apprehension is distinguished from inert things precisely in not being circumscribable.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** Na parimīyate is not a scientific theorem that awareness can never be quantified by any operational metric. The verse asserts a philosophical distinction concerning self-disclosure.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

The paired 8a/8b becomes a miniature argument: first a proposed distinguishing mark, then the contrast. The ablative jaḍāt is crucial to its structure.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
