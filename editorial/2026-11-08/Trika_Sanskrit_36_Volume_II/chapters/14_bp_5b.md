# 14. Nothing too small or too great

**Stage 3: What does Śakti actually do?**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Bodhapañcadaśikā* 5b**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

पूर्णत्वं सर्वभवेषु यस्या नाल्पं न चाधिकम्

*pūrṇatvaṃ sarvabhaveṣu yasyā nālpaṃ na cādhikam*

### The complete verse in context

तस्यैवैषा परा देवी स्वरूपामर्शनोत्सुका । पूर्णत्वं सर्वभवेषु यस्या नाल्पं न चाधिकम् ॥

*tasyaivaiṣā parā devī svarūpāmarśanotsukā | pūrṇatvaṃ sarvabhaveṣu yasyā nālpaṃ na cādhikam ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** पूर्ण uses ṇ (retroflex), not dental न. भावे versus भवे matters: long ा changes lexical identity.

| *yasyā* | यस्या | ya = य · syā = स्या |

These are **orthographic reading units** to help you construct Devanāgarī, not a metrical scansion. When a consonant ends a word without an inherent vowel, notice its virāma. Trace the conjuncts rather than inserting English-style epenthetic vowels.

Write the selected IAST in Devanāgarī using the reference sheet. Check consonants, vowel lengths and the presence of anusvāra (ं), visarga (ः) and virāma (्). It is fine to consult the chart; the point is a repeatable skill, not instant recognition.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **pūrṇatvam** *(recovered)* | pūrṇa + -tva | neuter nominative/accusative singular abstract noun | completeness, fullness |
| **sarvabhaveṣu** | sarva + bhava | masculine locative plural | in all modes of becoming |
| **yasyā** | yasyā [from yasyāḥ] | feminine genitive singular relative pronoun | of whom / whose |
| **na** | na | negation | not |
| **alpam** *(recovered)* | alpa | neuter adjective/substantive singular | little, scant |
| **ca** *(recovered)* | ca | coordinating particle | and |
| **adhikam** *(recovered)* | adhika | neuter singular | excessive, more |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **pūrṇatvam** = completeness, fullness · **sarvabhaveṣu** = in all modes of becoming · **yasyā** = of whom · **na** = not · **alpam** = little, scant · **ca** = and · **adhikam** = excessive, more.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. pūrṇa + -tva → pūrṇatva
2. bhava + locative plural -eṣu → bhaveṣu
3. yasyāḥ + na → yasyā na (visarga before voiced nasal disappears)
4. na + alpam → nālpam (a + a → ā)
5. ca + adhikam → cādhikam (a + a → ā)

**What the derivation demonstrates.** A lesson in how tiny vowels change meanings and in the ethical limits of metaphysical language.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** Her fullness in all forms of becoming is neither deficient nor excessive.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** In everything that comes into being, her fullness admits neither too little nor too much.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** Bhava has short a in the printed reading sarvabhaveṣu. Do not silently change it to bhāveṣu, even if English “states” is attractive. The line is dependent on the preceding reference to devī.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

One can contemplate fullness without treating it as proof that individual suffering, scarcity or injustice are unreal. The text describes an ultimate power; it does not erase empirical differences.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
