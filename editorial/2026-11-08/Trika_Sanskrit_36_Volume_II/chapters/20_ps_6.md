# 20. The spotless crystal takes colours

**Stage 4: The world in the sentence**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Paramārthasāra* 6**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

नानाविधवर्णानां रूपं धत्ते यथामलः स्फटिकः

*nānāvidhavarṇānāṃ rūpaṃ dhatte yathāmalaḥ sphaṭikaḥ*

### The complete verse in context

नानाविधवर्णानां रूपं धत्ते यथामलः स्फटिकः । सुरमानुषपशुपादपरूपत्वं तद्वद् ईशोऽपि ॥

*nānāvidhavarṇānāṃ rūpaṃ dhatte yathāmalaḥ sphaṭikaḥ | suramānuṣapaśupādaparūpatvaṃ tadvad īśo ’pi ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** स्फ = स् + फ; टि uses a short i sign that appears visually to the left; यथामलः contains contraction of final ā + initial a.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **nānāvidhavarṇānām** *(recovered)* | nānā + vidha + varṇa | masculine genitive plural compound | of many kinds of colours / appearances |
| **rūpam** *(recovered)* | rūpa | neuter accusative singular | form, appearance |
| **dhatte** | √dhā | present middle third singular | takes on, assumes, holds |
| **yathā** | yathā | comparative particle | just as |
| **amalaḥ** *(recovered)* | a-mala | masculine nominative singular | unstained, spotless |
| **sphaṭikaḥ** | sphaṭika | masculine nominative singular | crystal |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **nānāvidhavarṇānām** = of many kinds of colours · **rūpam** = form, appearance · **dhatte** = takes on, assumes, holds · **yathā** = just as · **amalaḥ** = unstained, spotless · **sphaṭikaḥ** = crystal.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. nānā + vidha + varṇa → nānāvidhavarṇa, genitive plural -ānām
2. yathā + amalaḥ → yathāmalaḥ (ā + a → ā)
3. a- + mala forms amala “without stain”
4. dhatte is a conjugated verb (√dhā), not a sandhi of “dhat” with “te”

**What the derivation demonstrates.** A visual analogy trains the reader to infer grammatical roles before importing philosophical theory.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** A spotless crystal takes on the appearance of various colours.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** Just as a clear crystal presents the forms of many colours without itself becoming their pigments.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** A literal translation says the crystal “assumes form,” not that light physics has proved a metaphysical doctrine. The next pāda applies the comparison to the Lord taking divine, human and other forms.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

This image is a controlled analogy: the variety of appearances does not establish a permanent plurality of separate essences. But the analogy should not flatten the real experience of difference.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
