# 15. A definition of inertness

**Stage 3: What does Śakti actually do?**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Bodhapañcadaśikā* 8a**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

परिच्छिन्नप्रकाशत्वं जडस्य किल लक्षणम्

*paricchinnaprakāśatvaṃ jaḍasya kila lakṣaṇam*

### The complete verse in context

परिच्छिन्नप्रकाशत्वं जडस्य किल लक्षणम् । जडाद् विलक्षणो बोधो यतो न परिमीयते ॥

*paricchinnaprakāśatvaṃ jaḍasya kila lakṣaṇam | jaḍād vilakṣaṇo bodho yato na parimīyate ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** परिच्छिन्न includes च्छ and न्न conjuncts. प्रकाश has प्र = प् + र; जड uses ड, not द.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **paricchinnaprakāśatvam** *(recovered)* | pari-√chid + prakāśa + -tva | neuter nominative singular abstract compound | having delimited manifestation / limited luminosity |
| **jaḍasya** | jaḍa | masculine or neuter genitive singular | of what is inert |
| **kila** | kila | indeclinable emphasis | indeed, so it is said |
| **lakṣaṇam** | lakṣaṇa | neuter nominative singular | defining characteristic, distinguishing mark |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **paricchinnaprakāśatvam** = having delimited manifestation · **jaḍasya** = of what is inert · **kila** = indeed, so it is said · **lakṣaṇam** = defining characteristic, distinguishing mark.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. pari-√chid (“cut around”) yields paricchinna, a past participial adjective “delimited”
2. prakāśa “shining/manifestation”
3. compound paricchinna-prakāśa + -tva creates abstract “limited-manifestness”
4. jaḍa + -sya yields genitive

**What the derivation demonstrates.** Theory enters as an explicit definition; learners see why technical nouns cannot be translated lazily.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** Limited manifestation is, indeed, the characteristic of what is inert.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** The mark of inertness, he says, is circumscribed disclosure.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** Prakāśa can be physical light, appearance or self-disclosure. Translating every instance as “consciousness” before the argument develops would beg the question.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

Abhinavagupta sets up a contrast, not an empirical test of neural correlates. Read the next lesson before concluding what he intends by the word jaḍa.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
