# 19. The bound experiencer is Śiva

**Stage 4: The world in the sentence**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Paramārthasāra* 5**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

भोक्ता च तत्र देही शिव एव गृहीतपशुभावः

*bhoktā ca tatra dehī śiva eva gṛhītapaśubhāvaḥ*

### The complete verse in context

तत्रान्तर् विश्वम् इदं विचित्रतनुकरणभुवनसंतानम् । भोक्ता च तत्र देही शिव एव गृहीतपशुभावः ॥

*tatrāntar viśvam idaṃ vicitratanukaraṇabhuvanasaṃtānam | bhoktā ca tatra dehī śiva eva gṛhītapaśubhāvaḥ ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** भोक्ता has long ā at the end but masculine nominative; देही has long ī and is likewise masculine. Script shape alone does not assign gender.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bhoktā** | bhoktṛ (√bhuj + agent suffix -tṛ) | masculine nominative singular | experiencer, enjoyer, one who undergoes |
| **ca** | ca | conjunctive indeclinable | and |
| **tatra** | tatra | locative adverb | there |
| **dehī** | dehin (deha + -in) | masculine nominative singular | embodied being |
| **śiva** | śiva [from śivaḥ] | masculine nominative singular | Śiva |
| **eva** | eva | emphatic particle | indeed, himself |
| **gṛhītapaśubhāvaḥ** | gṛhīta + paśu + bhāva | masculine nominative singular possessive compound | having assumed the condition of a bound being |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **bhoktā** = experiencer, enjoyer, one who undergoes · **ca** = and · **tatra** = there · **dehī** = embodied being · **śiva** = Śiva · **eva** = indeed, himself · **gṛhītapaśubhāvaḥ** = having assumed the condition of a bound being.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. bhoktṛ → bhoktā (masculine -tṛ nominative sg)
2. dehin → dehī (masculine -in nominative sg)
3. śivaḥ + eva → śiva eva (aḥ + vowel e
4. visarga is dropped in this environment)
5. gṛhīta (from √grah) + paśu + bhāva creates a compound ending -aḥ

**What the derivation demonstrates.** The deep claim of Śaiva nonduality can now be reconstructed from morphology rather than a convenient English translation.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** And the embodied experiencer there is Śiva himself, having taken on the state of a bound subject.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** The one who experiences the world in a body is none other than Śiva appearing in the condition of a limited being.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** Paśu in this Śaiva technical context means the bound individual; translating it as simply “animal” misses the doctrinal contrast to pati (Lord). The compound describes an adopted condition, not proof of an independently existing second entity.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

The accusative and nominative machinery of the previous lessons now culminates in an identification: an embodied agent is grammatically re-described as Śiva. This is an ontological interpretation, not a denial that individual choices have consequences.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
