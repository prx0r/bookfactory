# 34. Power that is knowing and known

**Stage 6: What persists?**  
**Kallaṭa / Vasugupta tradition; exact authorship debated · *Spandakārikā* 1.18**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

ज्ञानज्ञेयस्वरूपिण्या शक्त्या परमया युतः

*jñānajñeyasvarūpiṇyā śaktyā paramayā yutaḥ*

### The complete verse in context

ज्ञानज्ञेयस्वरूपिण्या शक्त्या परमया युतः । पदद्वये विभुर् भाति तदन्यत्र तु चिन्मयः ॥

*jñānajñeyasvarūpiṇyā śaktyā paramayā yutaḥ | padadvaye vibhur bhāti tadanyatra tu cinmayaḥ ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** ज्ञा = ज्+ञा; ज्ञेय has े; शक्ति uses क्त; स्वरूपिण्या combines ण्य + ा.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **jñānajñeyasvarūpiṇyā** | jñāna + jñeya + svarūpin | feminine instrumental singular long compound | having the forms of knowing and what is known |
| **śaktyā** | śakti | feminine instrumental singular | by / with power |
| **paramayā** | parama | feminine instrumental singular | supreme |
| **yutaḥ** | √yuj + -ta | masculine nominative singular past passive participle | joined, furnished, endowed |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **jñānajñeyasvarūpiṇyā** = having the forms of knowing and what is known · **śaktyā** = by · **paramayā** = supreme · **yutaḥ** = joined, furnished, endowed.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. jñāna + jñeya (√jñā + gerundive -ya) + svarūpin → jñānajñeyasvarūpin, feminine form svarūpiṇī
2. instrumental feminine → svarūpiṇyā
3. śakti + instr.sg -yā → śaktyā
4. parama + feminine instr -ayā → paramayā

**What the derivation demonstrates.** A precise capstone on feminine declensions, instrumental functions and the limits of extracting a single half-verse.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** Endowed with the supreme power whose forms are knowledge and the knowable…

**2. Another defensible rendering:** The Lord, joined with the highest Śakti that presents both cognition and its object…

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** This is **not a sentence by itself**: vibhur bhāti in the next half provides the subject and finite verb. Nor does instrumental śaktyā imply there are two absolutely independent realities.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

The grammatical agreement among three feminine instrumentals shows a single descriptive complex. Philosophically, knowing and known appear as two aspects of one power.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
