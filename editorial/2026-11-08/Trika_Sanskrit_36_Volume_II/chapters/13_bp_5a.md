# 13. The goddess longs to recognise herself

**Stage 3: What does Śakti actually do?**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Bodhapañcadaśikā* 5a**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

तस्यैवैषा परा देवी स्वरूपामर्शनोत्सुका

*tasyaivaiṣā parā devī svarūpāmarśanotsukā*

### The complete verse in context

तस्यैवैषा परा देवी स्वरूपामर्शनोत्सुका । पूर्णत्वं सर्वभवेषु यस्या नाल्पं न चाधिकम् ॥

*tasyaivaiṣā parā devī svarūpāmarśanotsukā | pūrṇatvaṃ sarvabhaveṣu yasyā nālpaṃ na cādhikam ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** तस्यै = त + स्य + ै; एषा begins with ए; आमर्शन is an ā-initial word embedded in a compound; त्सु is त् + सु.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **tasyaiva** | tasya + eva | genitive masculine/neuter singular demonstrative + emphatic particle | of that very one |
| **eṣā** *(recovered)* | eṣā | feminine nominative singular demonstrative | this [female] |
| **parā** | para | feminine nominative singular | supreme, highest |
| **devī** | devī | feminine nominative singular | goddess |
| **svarūpāmarśanotsukā** | sva + rūpa + āmarśana + utsuka | feminine nominative singular compound | eager to apprehend/reflect upon her own nature |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **tasyaiva** = of that very one · **eṣā** = this [female] · **parā** = supreme, highest · **devī** = goddess · **svarūpāmarśanotsukā** = eager to apprehend/reflect upon her own nature.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. tasya + eva → tasyaiva (a + e → ai)
2. tasyaiva + eṣā → tasyaivaiṣā (a + e → ai across next boundary)
3. sva + rūpa → svarūpa
4. āmarśana + utsuka → āmarśanotsuka (a + u → o)
5. utsuka + feminine -ā → utsukā

**What the derivation demonstrates.** A grammatical doorway into the heart of Śiva–Śakti metaphysics without collapsing translation into doctrine.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** This is the supreme goddess of that very one, eager to apprehend her own nature.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** His supreme power is this goddess, intent upon her own self-awareness.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** Āmarśana means reflective apprehension, contact or deliberation; rendering it as “self-reflexive consciousness” is legitimate philosophical explanation but not a one-word dictionary translation.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

Trika often pairs prakāśa (illumination) and vimarśa (self-apprehension). The feminine nominatives show that the goddess is the grammatical subject, not a passive instrument.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
