# 21. The mirror of the intellect

**Stage 4: The world in the sentence**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Paramārthasāra* 8**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

सर्वगतोऽप्य् अयम् आत्मा विषयाश्रयणेन धीमुकुरे

*sarvagato 'py ayam ātmā viṣayāśrayaṇena dhīmukure*

### The complete verse in context

राहुर् अदृश्योऽपि यथा शशिबिम्बस्थः प्रकाशते तद्वत् । सर्वगतोऽप्य् अयम् आत्मा विषयाश्रयणेन धीमुकुरे ॥

*rāhur adṛśyo 'pi yathā śaśibimbasthaḥ prakāśate tadvat | sarvagato 'py ayam ātmā viṣayāśrayaṇena dhīmukure ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** सर्वगतोऽप्ययम्: अवग्रह ऽ indicates an elided vowel; धी has long ī; मुकुरे locative e.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **sarvagato** | sarva + gata (√gam past participle) | masculine nominative singular | gone everywhere, omnipresent |
| **’py** *(recovered)* | api | indeclinable concessive | even though |
| **ayam** | idam | masculine nominative singular demonstrative | this |
| **ātmā** | ātman | masculine nominative singular | Self |
| **viṣayāśrayaṇena** | viṣaya + āśrayaṇa | neuter instrumental singular compound | by relying on objects |
| **dhīmukure** | dhī + mukura | masculine locative singular compound | in the mirror of the intellect |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **sarvagato** = gone everywhere, omnipresent · **’py** = even though · **ayam** = this · **ātmā** = Self · **viṣayāśrayaṇena** = by relying on objects · **dhīmukure** = in the mirror of the intellect.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. sarvagataḥ + api + ayam → sarvagato ’py ayam (aḥ+a → o plus avagraha
2. i+a → y+a)
3. viṣaya + āśrayaṇa → viṣayāśrayaṇa (a+ā→ā)
4. āśrayaṇa + ena → āśrayaṇena instrumental sg
5. dhī + mukura → dhīmukura, locative -e

**What the derivation demonstrates.** An expert skill: recover a verb from its earlier comparative clause rather than hallucinating one into the selected phrase.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** This Self, although present everywhere, [appears] in the mirror of intellect through dependence on objects.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** The all-pervading Self becomes manifest in the intellect’s mirror as it takes objects for support.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** The word “appears” is **supplied from prakāśate in the first half of the complete verse**; it is not a finite verb written in this excerpt. A translation must openly mark such elliptical syntax.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

An image of Rāhu appearing when connected with the moon precedes this line. The point concerns manifestness, not a literal claim about an invisible astronomical body inhabiting consciousness.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
