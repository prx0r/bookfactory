# 36. Bondage and freedom, read exactly

**Stage 6: What persists?**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Bodhapañcadaśikā* 14**  
**COMPLETE SŪTRA / VERSE**

## The Sanskrit

एतौ बन्धविमोक्षौ च परमेशस्वरूपतः । न भिद्येते न भेदो हि तत्त्वतः परमेश्वरे ॥

*etau bandhavimokṣau ca parameśasvarūpataḥ | na bhidyete na bhedo hi tattvataḥ parameśvare ||*

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** एतौ and मोक्षौ share the औ sign; भिद्येते has द् + य and long e; तत्त्वतः uses त्त्व and final visarga.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **etau** | etad | masculine nominative dual demonstrative | these two |
| **bandhavimokṣau** | bandha + vimokṣa | masculine nominative dual dvandva compound | bondage and release |
| **ca** | ca | coordinating particle | and |
| **parameśasvarūpataḥ** | parameśa + svarūpa + -tas | adverbial ablatival compound | from the standpoint of the Supreme Lord’s own nature |
| **na** | na | negation | not |
| **bhidyete** | √bhid | present passive third dual | are differentiated, divided |
| **bhedo** | bheda | masculine nominative singular | difference |
| **hi** | hi | particle | indeed |
| **tattvataḥ** | tattva + -tas | adverbial ablatival | in reality, from the standpoint of truth |
| **parameśvare** | parameśvara | masculine locative singular | in the supreme Lord |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **etau** = these two · **bandhavimokṣau** = bondage and release · **ca** = and · **parameśasvarūpataḥ** = from the standpoint of the Supreme Lord’s own nature · **na** = not · **bhidyete** = are differentiated, divided · **bhedo** = difference · **hi** = indeed · **tattvataḥ** = in reality, from the standpoint of truth · **parameśvare** = in the supreme Lord.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. etad → etau (masculine nominative dual)
2. bandha + vimokṣa → bandhavimokṣa, dual -au
3. √bhid passive present third dual → bhidyete (not 3rd plural bhidyante)
4. bhedaḥ + hi → bhedo hi (aḥ→o before h)
5. parameśvara + -e → parameśvare locative

**What the derivation demonstrates.** The final lesson is a full verse, and the resolution comes from grammatical reconstruction, not faith in an English aphorism.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** These two—bondage and liberation—are not differentiated from the standpoint of the Supreme Lord’s own nature; for in ultimate reality there is no difference in the Lord.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** Viewed through the Lord’s essential being, bondage and release are not ultimately two separate realities.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** **The standpoint is essential.** The verse is not asserting that a suffering bound subject experiences the same condition as one who has recognized freedom. Parameśasvarūpataḥ and tattvataḥ restrict the claim to the ultimate perspective.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

Everything learned returns: dual number (etau, -au, bhidyete), nominal predicates (bhedaḥ), locative (parameśvare), two ablatival adverbs (-taḥ) and sandhi. Once the derivation is clear, the reader may decide how far the nondual claim reaches.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
