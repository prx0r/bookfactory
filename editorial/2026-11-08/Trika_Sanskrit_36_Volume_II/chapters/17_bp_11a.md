# 17. Even not-knowing is named

**Stage 3: What does Śakti actually do?**  
**Abhinavagupta · *Bodhapañcadaśikā* 11a**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

यद् एतस्यापरिज्ञानं तत्स्वातन्त्र्यं हि वर्णितम्

*yad etasyāparijñānaṃ tatsvātantryaṃ hi varṇitam*

### The complete verse in context

यद् एतस्यापरिज्ञानं तत्स्वातन्त्र्यं हि वर्णितम् । स एव खलु संसारो मूडानां यो विभीषकः ॥

*yad etasyāparijñānaṃ tatsvātantryaṃ hi varṇitam | sa eva khalu saṃsāro mūḍānāṃ yo vibhīṣakaḥ ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** अपरिज्ञान has an initial privative अ; स्वतन्त्र्य contains त्र्य = त् + र् + य; वर्णितम् contains retroflex ण.

| IAST word | Devanāgarī word | Reading units (IAST = script) |
|---|---|---|
| *yad* | यद् | ya = य · d = द् |
| *etasyāparijñānaṃ* | एतस्यापरिज्ञानं | e = ए · ta = त · syā = स्या · pa = प · ri = रि · jñā = ज्ञा · naṃ = नं |
| *tatsvātantryaṃ* | तत्स्वातन्त्र्यं | ta = त · tsvā = त्स्वा · ta = त · ntryaṃ = न्त्र्यं |

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **yat** *(recovered)* | yad | neuter relative nominative singular | that which |
| **etasya** *(recovered)* | idam/etad | genitive masculine/neuter singular | of this one |
| **aparijñānam** *(recovered)* | a- + pari-√jñā + -ana | neuter nominative singular | non-recognition, lack of full understanding |
| **tat** | tad | neuter correlative nominative singular | that |
| **svātantryam** *(recovered)* | svatantra + -ya | neuter nominative singular | independence / freedom |
| **hi** | hi | indeclinable | indeed / for |
| **varṇitam** | √varṇ + -ta | neuter nominative singular past passive participle | described, stated |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **yat** = that which · **etasya** = of this one · **aparijñānam** = non-recognition, lack of full understanding · **tat** = that · **svātantryam** = independence · **hi** = indeed · **varṇitam** = described, stated.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. etasya + aparijñānam → etasyāparijñānam (a+a→ā)
2. tat + svātantryam → tatsvātantryam (t+s remains ts sequence)
3. svatantra → svātantrya is a lexical derivative with vowel gradation, not ordinary external sandhi
4. varṇita + m → varṇitam

**What the derivation demonstrates.** Introduces relative pronouns and avoids the mystic shorthand “ignorance is freedom” without qualifiers.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** What is non-recognition of this one has indeed been described as that very freedom.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** Even the failure to recognise him is spoken of as an exercise of his sovereignty.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** This is one of the most easily mishandled statements: the metaphysical inclusion of ignorance under divine freedom is not the practical claim that ignorance is desirable or harmless.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

The relative–correlative yad ... tat is a Sanskrit argumentative structure. It says something startling, but only after you parse what “that” refers to.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
