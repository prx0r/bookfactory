# 30. Devotion becomes a science

**Stage 5: Devotion without falsifying the grammar**  
**Utpaladeva · *Śivastotrāvalī* 1.12**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

भवद्भक्तिमहाविद्या येषाम् अभ्यासम् आगता

*bhavadbhaktimahāvidyā yeṣām abhyāsam āgatā*

### The complete verse in context

भवद्भक्तिमहाविद्या येषाम् अभ्यासम् आगता । विद्याविद्योभयस्यापि ता एते तत्त्ववेदिनः ॥

*bhavadbhaktimahāvidyā yeṣām abhyāsam āgatā | vidyāvidyobhayasyāpi tā ete tattvavedinaḥ ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** विद्या includes द् + य; अभ्यास has भ्य; आगता begins with independent long आ.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **bhavadbhaktimahāvidyā** | bhavat + bhakti + mahā + vidyā | feminine nominative singular compound | great knowledge / wisdom of devotion to you |
| **yeṣām** | yad | genitive masculine/neuter plural relative | of whom, whose |
| **abhyāsam** | abhyāsa | masculine accusative singular | exercise, repeated cultivation, practice |
| **āgatā** | ā-√gam + -ta | feminine nominative singular past participle | has come, entered, arrived |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **bhavadbhaktimahāvidyā** = great knowledge · **yeṣām** = of whom, whose · **abhyāsam** = exercise, repeated cultivation, practice · **āgatā** = has come, entered, arrived.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. bhavat + bhakti yields bhavad-bhakti through voicing of final t before voiced bh
2. mahā + vidyā → mahāvidyā
3. abhi + √as yields abhyāsa as lexical noun (do not pretend this is ordinary a+a sandhi)
4. āgatā feminine nominative agrees with vidyā

**What the derivation demonstrates.** Makes the reader more faithful to poetry by refusing to stop a Sanskrit relative clause prematurely.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** Those into whose practice the great science of devotion to You has come…

**2. Another defensible rendering:** People whose ongoing discipline has been entered by the great knowledge of devotion to You…

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** A complete translation requires the next half-verse: these are knowers of the reality of both knowledge and ignorance. The first half is an unfinished relative clause.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

Here the book teaches restraint. We can love a resonant half-line while admitting that the syntax needs its continuation. Devotion is treated as a vidyā, a disciplined knowing, not mere intensity of feeling.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
