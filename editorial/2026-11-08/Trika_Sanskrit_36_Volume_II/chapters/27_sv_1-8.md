# 27. Visible amid the turmoil of knowing

**Stage 5: Devotion without falsifying the grammar**  
**Utpaladeva · *Śivastotrāvalī* 1.8**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

वेद्यवेदकसंक्षोभेऽप्यसि भक्तैः सुदर्शनः

*vedyavedakasaṃkṣobhe 'py asi bhaktaiḥ sudarśanaḥ*

### The complete verse in context

नाथ वेद्यक्षये केन न दृश्योऽस्य् एककः स्थितः । वेद्यवेदकसंक्षोभेऽप्यसि भक्तैः सुदर्शनः ॥

*nātha vedyakṣaye kena na dṛśyo 'sy ekakaḥ sthitaḥ | vedyavedakasaṃkṣobhe 'py asi bhaktaiḥ sudarśanaḥ ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** वे द्य has द् + य; संक्षोभे contains क्ष; भक्तैः has diphthong ai and visarga.

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **vedyavedakasaṃkṣobhe** | vedya + vedaka + saṃkṣobha | masculine locative singular compound | amid the agitation of known and knower |
| **’py** *(recovered)* | api | concessive indeclinable | even |
| **asi** | √as | present active second singular | you are |
| **bhaktaiḥ** | bhakta | masculine instrumental plural | by devotees |
| **sudarśanaḥ** | su + darśana | masculine nominative singular adjective | readily visible, easy to behold |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **vedyavedakasaṃkṣobhe** = amid the agitation of known and knower · **’py** = even · **asi** = you are · **bhaktaiḥ** = by devotees · **sudarśanaḥ** = readily visible, easy to behold.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. vedya (what is to be known) + vedaka (knower) + saṃkṣobha → compound with -e locative
2. saṃkṣobhe + api + asi → saṃkṣobhe ’py asi (a of api elided after e, i becomes y before a)
3. bhakta + -aiḥ → bhaktaiḥ instrumental plural
4. su + darśana → sudarśanaḥ

**What the derivation demonstrates.** An advanced but rewarding demonstration of multiple sandhis and agentive instrumentals.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** Even amid the turbulence of known and knower, you are readily seen by devotees.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** To devotees, You remain visible even where knowing and its object seem locked in agitation.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** Sudārśana should not be printed: the source gives sudarśana (short a). The “devotee sees” assertion is theological; it does not mandate ocular visions.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

The perceiver–perceived distinction is not erased by refusing to notice objects. The devotional gaze understands the play of cognition as the arena of disclosure.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
