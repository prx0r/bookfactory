# 33. Actions perish; agency does not

**Stage 6: What persists?**  
**Kallaṭa / Vasugupta tradition; exact authorship debated · *Spandakārikā* 1.14**  
**EXTRACT — read alongside its complete source verse**

## The Sanskrit

कार्यता क्षयिणी तत्र कर्तृत्वं पुनर् अक्षयम्

*kāryatā kṣayiṇī tatra kartṛtvaṃ punar akṣayam*

### The complete verse in context

अवस्थायुगलं चात्र कार्यकर्तृत्वशब्दितम् । कार्यता क्षयिणी तत्र कर्तृत्वं पुनर् अक्षयम् ॥

*avasthāyugalaṃ cātra kāryakartṛtvaśabditam | kāryatā kṣayiṇī tatra kartṛtvaṃ punar akṣayam ||*

The larger verse is supplied so that the excerpt never masquerades as an independently transmitted mantra or complete proposition. The lesson analyses the **selected passage** word by word; a later volume can analyse the entire surrounding stanza.

## Before seeing the English

Read the Sanskrit aloud. Cover the translation below. Determine where one word ends, whether any final form has changed through sandhi, and which word is acting as subject or predicate. Ask what the grammar **requires** and what remains open.

**Writing laboratory.** कार्य contains र् + य, कर्तृत्व contains त् + ऋ and त् + व (different vowel/conjunct mechanisms).

## Word-by-word anatomy

| Visible / reconstructed form | Lemma / segmentation | Exact grammatical role | What it can mean |
|---|---|---|---|
| **kāryatā** | kārya (√kṛ, gerundive) + -tā | feminine nominative singular abstract noun | condition of being effected / of being a product |
| **kṣayiṇī** | kṣayin | feminine nominative singular | perishable, subject to waning |
| **tatra** | tatra | adverb | there, in that pair |
| **kartṛtvam** *(recovered)* | kartṛ (agent of √kṛ) + -tva | neuter nominative singular | agency, capacity of being an agent |
| **punar** | punar | adversative indeclinable | but, however |
| **akṣayam** | a- + kṣaya | neuter nominative singular | imperishable, not subject to loss |

**Reader’s reconstruction:** **kāryatā** = condition of being effected · **kṣayiṇī** = perishable, subject to waning · **tatra** = there, in that pair · **kartṛtvam** = agency, capacity of being an agent · **punar** = but, however · **akṣayam** = imperishable, not subject to loss.  Notice how this gives you building blocks rather than an English sentence. Only after identifying the inflections and the omitted or explicit verb should you commit to the literal wording.


## Derivation: reverse it, then rebuild it

1. kārya + -tā → kāryatā (abstract feminine)
2. kṣayin + feminine -ī → kṣayiṇī
3. kartṛ + -tva → kartṛtva
4. a- + kṣaya → akṣaya, neuter sg akṣayam
5. kāryatā with kṣayiṇī is feminine agreement

**What the derivation demonstrates.** The sharp distinction between a product and an agent is actually legible in Sanskrit morphology.

Before moving on, reverse at least one of the displayed transformations without looking. Distinguish an external sandhi rule (changes at a word boundary) from a suffix or inflection rule (formation of an individual word). They are not interchangeable explanations.

## Translation laboratory

**1. A conservative literal reconstruction:** The condition of being an effect is perishable; agency, however, is imperishable.

**2. Another defensible rendering:** Products cease; the fundamental capacity for agency is not exhausted by any particular deed.

**3. What the Sanskrit does *not* automatically guarantee:** The verse’s “agency” is a technical philosophical capacity, not a claim that an individual body can perform without limits or never die. Keep the antecedent “two conditions” from the first half in view.

These alternatives are not votes on what to believe. Return to the actual case endings, verb voice and contextual antecedents. If the text permits more than one English formulation, learn what the *Sanskrit* resolves and what the interpretation must supply.

## The Trika turn

Compare what comes and goes with the capacity from which actions issue. There is a deliberate shift from feminine kāryatā to neuter kartṛtvam; the agreement marks two distinct propositions.

This is a philosophical reading of the passage within nondual Śaivism. Where a doctrine is supplied by the surrounding text or commentarial tradition, it is not a hidden morpheme in the Sanskrit. Grammar determines what is said; philosophy asks what follows.
