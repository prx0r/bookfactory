# Editorial title and sales copy — proposed, not a final jacket

## Title

**THIRTY-SIX WINDOWS ON CONSCIOUSNESS**  
*Learn Sanskrit by Taking the Words Apart*  
**Thirty-Six Trika Statements from the Śiva Sūtras, Abhinavagupta, Utpaladeva, and the Spanda Tradition**

## One-sentence promise

Thirty-six astonishing Sanskrit statements, dismantled until every letter, case ending, compound, verb and sandhi makes sense—and until you can decide what the ancient authors mean without asking a translation to do the thinking for you.

## Back-cover copy

**“Consciousness is the Self.”** Two words in Sanskrit. But which word is consciousness? Why does one end in *-am* and the other in *-ā*? Where is the word “is”? What does the sentence really establish?

Begin there. Then follow thirty-five more sentences through the language of Kashmir's nondual Śaiva thinkers. You will discover how a noun ending changes in front of another word; why an identical pronoun can mean “your” and “they”; how a single case ending says *in*, *from*, or *by*; and how Sanskrit poetry makes a claim more exact, not less, when its words are patiently unpacked.

Every chapter puts original Devanāgarī beside precise romanisation. It gives you the sounds, word-by-word morphology, reversible derivations, an honest literal translation, an alternative philosophical reading, and a test that proves whether you've understood the Sanskrit yourself.

The book does not require previous Devanāgarī knowledge or allegiance to any metaphysical conclusion. It asks only for attention. The reward is extraordinary: the poems and sūtras stop being beautiful but inaccessible quotations. They become sentences you can read, challenge, remember—and translate.

**No invented Sanskrit. No mystical etymologies passed off as grammar. No need to believe the translation before understanding the language.**

## Marketing caveat

Do not claim a certified critical edition, independent scholarly peer review, rights clearance, or a guaranteed 108-page format until the philological and KDP gates have actually passed. The manuscript is a 36-lesson English editorial draft.
