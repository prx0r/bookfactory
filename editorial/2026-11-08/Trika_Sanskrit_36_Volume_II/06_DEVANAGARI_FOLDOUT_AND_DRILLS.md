# Devanāgarī foldout — use this as a reference, not a preliminary exam

The reading practice is to **try** to reconstruct the Devanāgarī from IAST before looking at the printed line. Keep this sheet beside you. At first you may use it every few seconds. By the sixth or seventh lesson, you should begin to prefer the original script.

## The vowel inventory

| IAST | Independent letter | After क | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | अ | क | short *a*, inherent in a bare consonant |
| ā | आ | का | long *ā* |
| i | इ | कि | short *i*; sign written to the left of the consonant |
| ī | ई | की | long *ī* |
| u | उ | कु | short *u* |
| ū | ऊ | कू | long *ū* |
| ṛ | ऋ | कृ | syllabic *ṛ*, not “ri” with an English rolling r |
| ṝ | ॠ | कॄ | long syllabic *ṝ* (rare) |
| ḷ | ऌ | कॢ | syllabic *ḷ* (rare) |
| e | ए | के | long steady *e* |
| ai | ऐ | कै | diphthong *ai* |
| o | ओ | को | long steady *o* |
| au | औ | कौ | diphthong *au* |

The common mistake is to read कि as *ik*. Its shape appears leftward; its order in sound is **k then i**. Sanskrit vowels are measured by length, not an English-style stress accent.

## Consonants, in their traditional phonetic families

| Articulation | Unvoiced | Aspirated | Voiced | Voiced aspirated | Nasal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Velar (back of tongue) | क k | ख kh | ग g | घ gh | ङ ṅ |
| Palatal (hard palate) | च c | छ ch | ज j | झ jh | ञ ñ |
| Retroflex (tongue curled back) | ट ṭ | ठ ṭh | ड ḍ | ढ ḍh | ण ṇ |
| Dental (tongue near teeth) | त t | थ th | द d | ध dh | न n |
| Labial (lips) | प p | फ ph | ब b | भ bh | म m |

**Semivowels:** य y, र r, ल l, व v.  
**Sibilants:** श ś (palatal), ष ṣ (retroflex), स s (dental).  
**Breath consonant:** ह h.

*kh, gh, th, dh, ph* and their relatives are **single aspirated consonants** in this scheme, not a consonant followed by a separate *h* sound. Distinguish **थ th** from **ठ ṭh**; **द d** from **ड ḍ**; and **न n** from **ण ṇ**.

## Three signs you will see everywhere

| Sign | IAST | Function |
|---|---|---|
| ं | ṃ | anusvāra, a nasal indicator with context-sensitive realisation |
| ः | ḥ | visarga, a breath-like release or its sandhied reflex |
| ् | — | virāma, suppressing an inherent vowel; क् is *k*, not *ka* |
| ऽ | ’ | avagraha, ordinarily marking the elision of an initial a |

A final **म** means *ma* if no virāma is displayed. A final **म्** means *m*. A final **ं** represents anusvāra. Those three are not identical spellings.

## Learn the conjuncts you actually meet

| Visible | Components | IAST | Lesson |
|---|---|---|---|
| ज्ञ | ज् + ञ | jña | 2, 17, 31 |
| त्म | त् + म | tma | 1, 10 |
| न्त | न् + त | nta | 26 |
| न्ध | न् + ध | ndha | 26, 36 |
| द्य | द् + य | dya | 27, 36 |
| क्त | क् + त | kta | 30 |
| क्ष | क् + ष | kṣa | 9, 18, 23 |
| त्र | त् + र | tra | 24, 29 |
| श्र | श् + र | śra | later texts |
| त्त्व | त् + त् + व | ttva | 18, 36 |

The *repha* of र् before a following consonant may be drawn above the resulting cluster. Do not assume the vertical order of signs gives the reading order. Every sign is part of a left-to-right phoneme sequence, even when its glyph is visually above, below or before the consonant.

## Five two-minute drills, repeated across the volume

**Drill A — vowels.** Read क, का, कि, की, कु, कू, के, कै, को, कौ. Rewrite as IAST without peeking. Then reverse.

**Drill B — dental versus retroflex.** Write त-ट, द-ड, न-ण, स-ष. Say each pair slowly. Write both अनु and अणु and explain why the difference changes meaning.

**Drill C — find the *a*.** On a random line, point to every consonant with a bare inherent *a*. Then point to every vowel sign that overrides it.

**Drill D — reverse a sandhi.** Choose a verse containing -o: *udyamo*, *bodho*, *saṃvinmārgo*. Recover the nominative in -aḥ and rebuild the surface form.

**Drill E — one Sanskrit sentence from memory.** Pick the shortest line that you actually understand. Write its IAST, reconstruct its Devanāgarī, identify the noun endings, and then write **two English translations**: one conservative, one interpretative. Explain what extra interpretation enters the second.

## Optional Mātṛkā nyāsa study

The ordering of phonemes by place and manner of articulation is useful for pronunciation. Abhinavagupta’s theological account of the alphabet is a separate tradition of interpretation. Nyāsa (placement of letters on the body) should be taught from a named source, with a warning that Mātṛkā and Mālinī sequences differ.
