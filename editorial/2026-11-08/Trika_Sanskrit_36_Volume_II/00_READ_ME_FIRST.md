## Five autonomous agent tasks (in order)
1. **Freeze independent witnesses.** Select a rights-cleared print scan for each of the five works, collate all 36 selected quotations and full-verse contexts word-for-word; record edition, page and variants. Never silently fix a witness.
2. **Conduct an independent Sanskrit audit.** Every lemma, case, number, finite verb, suffix, compound and sandhi in the tables must be reviewed by a competent Sanskrit reader. 3. **Review the philosophy in context.** Compare relevant Kṣemarāja / Yogarāja interpretations and distinguish commentarial evidence from the draft's original contemplative synthesis. Keep the original complete verse beside extracts. Do not turn a proposition into a physical sensation claim.
4. **Proofread pedagogy with a true beginner.** Can they derive the visible Sanskrit from each step? Can they reconstruct it from IAST using the chart? Do multiple glosses actually fit the grammar? Fix any explanation that only makes sense if one already knows the conclusion.
5. **Produce the release edition.** Author-approve the English, secure textual copy rights, rebuild in bookfactory, verify Devanāgarī fonts, proofs, pagination, contents and KDP artifacts. This zip is content-only; no PDF/KDP approval is implied.

## Distinctiveness / reuse policy
These 36 reading selections deliberately avoid the original 10 lesson cores and the screenshot examples **satatoditaṃ**, **mokṣasya naiva...**, **ajñānagranthibhidā...** and *Śivastotrāvalī* 16.1. A few Sanskrit words recur (necessary for language acquisition), but these are new teaching passages. Overlap with complete-source reference books is allowed as **credited teaching quotation**, not as a competing claim to own a verse.

## The one-verse rule
An excerpt is labelled EXTRACT; the full source appears underneath so the reader can test whether the excerpt is a real proposition or an unfinished syntactic piece. If a line is too long to memorise, give an **anchor phrase** without claiming an independent transmitted mantra.
